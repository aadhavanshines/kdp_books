# Chapter 9: Admin and Operations

Admin is the invisible work that keeps a business running: checklists, procedures, supplier emails, schedules and the occasional form. None of it is hard; all of it takes time. These workflows turn it into minutes.

## Workflow 41: Write a Standard Operating Procedure

**Use it when:** a job is done differently every time, or only you know how to do it.

> **Time Saved:** About 1.5 hours per procedure (from 2 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Turn my rough description of how we [do the task] into a clear
standard operating procedure. Include: purpose, who does it, when,
what you need, numbered steps (one action each), common mistakes,
and how to check it was done right. Simple words a new hire
would understand. Here's how I do it:

[describe it, or paste a voice-note transcript]
```

```result
CLOSING THE SHOP
Purpose: leave the shop clean, safe and ready for the 4 a.m. bake.
Who: closing staff. When: 3 p.m. to 3:45 p.m.
1. Turn the door sign to "Closed" at 3 p.m.
2. Bag unsold bread for the food bank and label with the date.
3. Switch off the coffee machine and run the cleaning cycle.
...
Common mistakes: leaving the proofer on; forgetting the back door.
Check: the closing checklist is signed and on the clipboard.
```

**Make it better:** record yourself explaining the task while you do it, use the phone app's voice input or a transcript, and paste that in.

**Variations:**

- **Checklist version:** ask for the same procedure as a one-page checklist to pin on the wall.
- **Training quiz:** ask for five questions to check a new hire understands it.

## Workflow 42: Make an Opening and Closing Checklist

**Use it when:** small things get forgotten at the start or end of the day.

> **Time Saved:** About 40 minutes (from 50 down to 10).

```
Write opening and closing checklists for [business]. Things that
must happen: [list everything you can think of, in any order].
Group them by area, put them in a sensible order, and add anything
a business like mine commonly forgets (safety, security, cash,
equipment). Each list must fit on one printed page with check boxes.
```

```result
OPENING (6:30 a.m.)
FRONT OF HOUSE
[ ] Lights on, heating to 20°C
[ ] Cash in register: $150, counted and signed
[ ] Card reader on and tested
SAFETY
[ ] Fire exit clear and unlocked
[ ] First aid kit checked (weekly: Mondays)
```

**Make it better:** after a month, ask Claude to add anything that went wrong because it wasn't on the list.

**Variations:**

- **Mobile business:** ask for a "before the first job" van checklist.
- **Office or studio:** ask for a Friday shutdown checklist (backups, trash, alarms).

## Workflow 43: Email a Supplier

**Use it when:** you need to order, query a delivery, ask for a better price or raise a problem with a supplier.

> **Time Saved:** About 15 minutes per email (from 20 down to 5).

```
Write an email to my supplier [name] about [order / problem /
price]. Facts: [order number, dates, quantities, what went wrong].
What I want: [outcome]. Tone: [friendly and firm / just friendly].
Under 130 words, with a clear request and a date to reply by.
```

```result
Subject: Order 5531 - two sacks short

Hi Paula,

Tuesday's delivery (order 5531) was two 25 kg sacks of rye flour
short; the delivery note shows 10, we received 8.

Could you send the missing two with Friday's delivery, or credit
them on this month's invoice? Please let me know which by Thursday.

Thanks, as always, for the help.
Sam
```

**Make it better:** keep a short note of each supplier's quirks in your Project ("Paula prefers phone calls for urgent issues").

**Variations:**

- **Negotiating price:** ask for an email that asks for a volume discount and mentions your order history.
- **Finding a new supplier:** ask for an inquiry email listing your volumes, delivery needs and questions.

## Workflow 44: Plan a Staff Schedule

**Use it when:** building the weekly schedule takes an hour of juggling.

> **Time Saved:** About 45 minutes a week (from 1 hour down to 15).

```
Help me build next week's staff schedule. Opening hours: [hours]. Staff,
their contracted hours and availability: [list]. Rules: [minimum
staff per shift, breaks, who can open or close, max hours]. Busy
times: [when]. Give me a table by day and shift, the hours each
person gets, and any rule you couldn't meet.
```

```result
| Day | 6:30-11:00 | 11:00-15:30 |
| Tue | Sam, Leah | Maria, Tom |
| Wed | Sam, Tom | Leah, Maria |
...
Hours: Sam 36, Leah 24, Maria 24, Tom 20.
Couldn't meet: Saturday needs 3 staff in the morning but only Sam
and Leah are available. Consider asking Tom to swap Thursday.
```

> **Check First:** Check the schedule against employment rules where you work (breaks, rest periods, minors' hours) and each person's contract.

**Make it better:** keep each person's availability in a spreadsheet and attach it each week, instead of retyping it.

**Variations:**

- **Appointment business:** ask Claude to match staff skills to booked appointments.
- **Vacation cover:** ask for a plan that covers a staff member's two-week vacation fairly.

## Workflow 45: Write Clear Instructions for Customers

**Use it when:** customers need to follow steps: aftercare, setup, preparation for an appointment, how to use a product.

> **Time Saved:** About 1 hour (from 75 minutes down to 15).

```
Write simple instructions for customers on how to [task]. Who's
reading: [customer type]. What they need to know: [steps, warnings,
tips]. Numbered steps, one action per step, short sentences. Add a
"common questions" section with three questions. Under 300 words.
```

```result
CARING FOR YOUR CELEBRATION CAKE
1. Keep the box flat - in the car, put it on the floor, not a seat.
2. Store in a cool room (not the fridge) until 2 hours before
   serving.
3. To cut neat slices, dip your knife in hot water and wipe it dry
   between cuts.
Common questions:
How long will it keep? Best within 2 days...
```

**Make it better:** ask for a version with larger text and fewer words, to print on a card that goes in the box.

**Variations:**

- **Before an appointment:** ask for a "how to prepare" message sent the day before.
- **Product setup:** paste the manufacturer's manual and ask for a one-page quick-start.

## Workflow 46: Fill In and Understand Forms

**Use it when:** a long form, application or official letter needs filling in and you don't know what half the questions mean.

> **Time Saved:** About 40 minutes (from 1 hour down to 20).

```
I need to complete the form below for [purpose]. Please: 1) explain
in plain English what each section is asking for; 2) list the
documents and information I need to gather; 3) flag any section
where a mistake could be costly or where I should get advice.
Don't fill in personal or financial details for me.

[paste the form text or attach it, with personal details removed]
```

```result
SECTION 3: "Projected annual turnover"
Plain English: how much money you expect to take in sales this year,
before costs.
You'll need: last year's sales figure and your forecast.
SECTION 7: "Declaration of liability"
FLAG: this is a legal statement. Read it carefully, and ask your
accountant or adviser if unsure.
```

> **Check First:** For tax, legal, licensing or grant forms, use Claude to understand the questions, then confirm your answers with the issuing body or a professional.

**Make it better:** ask Claude to turn the document list into a checklist with a date to gather each item.

**Variations:**

- **Grant application:** see Workflow 98.
- **Tender or supplier form:** ask for draft answers to the descriptive questions, using your business profile.

## Workflow 47: Organize a Messy List

**Use it when:** you have a jumble of notes, tasks, ideas or contacts and need order.

> **Time Saved:** About 25 minutes (from 30 down to 5).

```
Here is a messy list of [tasks / notes / ideas / stock items].
Remove duplicates, group similar items, put each group in a
sensible order, and mark anything that looks urgent or unclear.
Output a clean list with headings.

[paste the list]
```

```result
URGENT
- Renew food hygiene certificate (expires Dec 1)
- Fix the walk-in fridge seal
SUPPLIERS
- Ask Mill Flour Co. about rye price
- New packaging quote from BoxRight
UNCLEAR
- "call about the thing on Tuesday" - which thing?
```

**Make it better:** take a photo of a whiteboard or handwritten list with the phone app and ask Claude to read it.

**Variations:**

- **Stock list:** ask for it sorted by supplier with quantities to reorder.
- **Contacts:** ask for a table with name, company, role and what they're for.

## Workflow 48: Plan an Event

**Use it when:** you are running an open day, launch, class, pop-up or workshop.

> **Time Saved:** About 2 hours (from 2.5 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Help me plan [event] on [date] at [place] for about [number] people.
Budget: [amount]. Goal: [goal]. Give me: a timeline of tasks from
now until the day, a run-of-show for the day itself (time, what
happens, who's responsible), a shopping and equipment list, a
simple budget table, and three ways to promote it.
```

```result
TIMELINE
4 weeks before: book the community hall; set ticket price...
2 weeks before: order ingredients; email the list...
THE DAY
9:00 Set up tables and stations (Sam, Leah)
10:00 Doors open - welcome coffee
10:15 Demonstration: shaping a loaf
...
BUDGET: ingredients $180, hall $120, aprons $90 = $390
```

**Make it better:** ask Claude for a "what could go wrong" list with a backup plan for each.

**Variations:**

- **Online event:** ask for a plan with the tech check, links and a host script.
- **Market stall:** ask for a packing list and a stall layout description.

## Workflow 49: Write a Business Email You've Been Putting Off

**Use it when:** there is one email you have avoided for days because it is awkward.

> **Time Saved:** About 30 minutes (from 40 down to 10), and the weight off your mind.

```
I need to write an email I've been putting off. Situation: [what's
going on, honestly]. Who it's to: [relationship]. What I want to
happen: [outcome]. What I'm worried about: [worry]. Draft it in a
clear, kind and direct tone, under 150 words. Then give me one
softer and one firmer version.
```

```result
"Hi Joe, I want to be upfront: after this month we won't be able to
continue the Wednesday delivery for the café. Our van is needed for
wholesale orders that day. I know that's a nuisance, so I'd like to
offer Tuesday or Thursday instead, at the same price..."
```

**Make it better:** read it aloud before sending. If it doesn't sound like you, ask Claude to use your own wording from a past email.

**Variations:**

- **Saying no to a request:** ask for a polite no with an alternative.
- **Ending a business relationship:** ask for a professional, clear and grateful version.

## Workflow 50: Write a Simple Contingency Plan

**Use it when:** you realize that if one thing broke (power, a key person, a supplier), you would have no plan.

> **Time Saved:** About 2 hours (from 2.5 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Help me write a simple contingency plan for [business]. Things that
would hurt us most: [for example key supplier fails, owner is ill,
power cut, website down, flood]. For each: early warning signs,
what to do in the first hour and the first week, who to contact,
and one thing to put in place now. Keep it to two pages.
```

```result
IF THE OWNER IS ILL FOR A WEEK
First hour: Leah opens with the standard menu; cake orders paused.
First week: Maria handles cake orders already booked...
Put in place now: give Leah a written list of supplier contacts and
the order log password (stored in the safe, not shared by email).
```

**Make it better:** print it, put it somewhere everyone knows, and review it every six months with Workflow 90.

**Variations:**

- **Cyber incident:** ask for a plan for a hacked email or website, with steps and contacts.
- **Peak season:** ask for a plan for a week when demand doubles.
