# Part III: Making It Stick

# Chapter 15: Building Your Toolkit

A hundred workflows are only useful if you can find the right one in ten seconds. This chapter shows how to turn the ones you use most into a small, organized toolkit: a few Projects, a set of saved prompts, and simple habits that make Claude feel like part of the business instead of a website you sometimes visit.

## Start With Five, Not a Hundred

Nobody uses a hundred workflows. Most businesses find that five to ten do most of the work. Go back through Part II and mark the workflows that match jobs you do every week. A typical first set looks something like this:

- **A retail shop or café:** Workflow 3 (plan a month of marketing), 12 (captions from a photo), 31 (answer a customer email), 33 (reply to reviews) and 81 (plan your day).
- **A trade or service business:** Workflow 23 (quote cover email), 26 (chase a late payment), 44 (staff rota), 72 (detailed quote) and 84 (meeting follow-up).
- **A consultant or freelancer:** Workflow 21 (cold email), 22 (follow-ups), 71 (proposal), 84 (meeting follow-up) and 90 (monthly review).

Chapter 18 has a longer list for each type of business. Use those five for two weeks before adding more. Habits beat collections.

## One Project per Area of Work

In Chapter 2 you created a Project with your business profile. That Project is your general workspace. As you use Claude more, it helps to create a few more, each with its own instructions and files:

| Project | Instructions and files to add |
| Marketing and social | Business profile, Brand Voice Guide, recent posts that did well |
| Customer service | Customer FAQ Sheet, Reply Rules, your returns policy |
| Money and admin | Your expense categories, price list, supplier list (no account numbers) |
| Team | Staff handbook sections, rota rules, onboarding plan |
| Planning | Quarterly goals, monthly reviews, annual plan |

Keep each Project focused. A customer service Project that only knows your FAQ and reply rules gives more reliable answers than one stuffed with every document you own.

> **Tip:** Put the instruction "If the answer is not in the files, say so and leave a [CHECK] note" in every Project that drafts messages for customers. It is the simplest way to stop Claude from guessing.

## Your Saved Prompt Library

Retyping prompts wastes the time you're trying to save. Keep your favorites somewhere you can copy them in seconds:

1. **In the Project itself.** Add a file called "Our prompts" with your five favorite workflows, already filled in with your details. Then you can type: "Use the review reply prompt on these three reviews."
2. **In a notes app or document.** One page, with a heading for each prompt. This works on your phone, too.
3. **In your email or helpdesk templates.** For customer replies, paste the finished saved replies (Workflow 36) into your email program, so the team can use them without opening Claude at all.

The bonus Prompt Library (Appendix A) has every prompt in this book in a single file, ready to copy into whichever of these you choose.

## Fill In the Brackets Once

Every prompt in this book has [brackets]. The first time you use one, you fill them in. Don't throw that work away. Save the filled-in version, and next time you only change what's new.

For example, the generic Workflow 26 prompt becomes your own:

```
Write three emails to chase an overdue invoice. Client: [name].
Invoice: [number], amount [amount], due [date]. Use our payment
terms: 30 days, late fee of 2% a month as stated on every invoice.
Email 1: friendly. Email 2: one week later, firmer. Email 3: two
weeks later, final notice saying we'll pause deliveries. Sign as
Sam, Northbrook Bakery.
```

Now only four brackets are left, and the emails will sound the same every time.

## Make Claude Ask the Questions

When a prompt has many brackets, you can let Claude do the work of asking. Add this line to the end of any workflow:

```
Before you start, ask me for any information you need, one question
at a time.
```

This turns a long form into a short conversation, which many people find easier, especially on a phone.

## Sharing the Toolkit With Your Team

If others in your business use Claude, consistency matters: customers should get the same answer whoever replies.

- **Shared Projects.** Team plans let you share Projects, so everyone works from the same instructions, files and voice. Check the current plan details on the Claude website.
- **One owner per Project.** Choose one person to keep each Project's files up to date. An out-of-date price list in a Project leads to out-of-date prices in replies.
- **The AI use policy.** Give everyone the policy you wrote in Chapter 4 (the bonus **AI Use Policy** template), and talk it through. The most important rules: no customer personal details unless your policy allows it, and a person checks everything before it goes out.
- **A short training session.** Use Workflow 70 to plan a 30-minute session using your own examples.

## Keep It Fresh

A toolkit decays when the business changes and the files don't. Set a reminder for the first of each month (when you do your monthly review, Workflow 90) to spend ten minutes on these checks:

- Are prices, hours and policies in every Project still correct?
- Did any reply go out with a mistake? If so, what instruction would have prevented it? Add that instruction.
- Which prompts did you use most? Move them to the top of your library.
- Which did you never use? Delete them. A short toolkit is a fast toolkit.

> **Remember:** The toolkit is not the goal. The goal is getting your evenings back. If a workflow doesn't save you time, drop it without guilt.
