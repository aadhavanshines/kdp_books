# Chapter 14: Research, Strategy and Growth

The last ten workflows step back from daily jobs to the bigger picture: understanding your market, your competitors and your customers, and planning how to grow. Claude is a patient thinking partner for this kind of work, and it is quick at turning scattered information into a clear picture.

> **Check First:** When Claude talks about markets, competitors or trends, ask where the information comes from. If your plan includes web search, ask it to search and give sources; otherwise its knowledge may be out of date. Check any fact before you rely on it.

## Workflow 91: Research Your Competitors

**Use it when:** you want to know what nearby or online competitors offer, charge and say about themselves.

> **Time Saved:** About 2 hours (from 2.5 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Help me understand my competitors. My business: [short description].
Competitors: [names, with notes or text copied from their websites,
menus or price lists]. Make a comparison table: what they offer,
prices, who they target, what they claim makes them special, and
their reviews' common praise and complaints. Then tell me: where
we're stronger, where we're weaker, and two gaps nobody is filling.
```

```result
| | Northbrook | Crumb & Co | SuperFresh |
| Sourdough loaf | $6.50 | $7.20 | $3.99 |
| Custom cakes | Yes | No | Basic only |
| Delivery | Wholesale | No | Yes |
GAPS NOBODY IS FILLING:
1. Gluten-free bread baked fresh locally
2. Weekday breakfast boxes for offices
```

**Make it better:** if your plan includes web search, ask Claude to look up each competitor and cite its sources, then check them yourself.

**Variations:**

- **Online sellers:** ask for a comparison of product listings, shipping and returns.
- **Watch list:** ask for a checklist to repeat every six months.

## Workflow 92: Find Out What Customers Really Want

**Use it when:** you want to understand your customers better before changing products, prices or marketing.

> **Time Saved:** About 1.5 hours (from 2 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Help me understand my customers. Here is what I have: [reviews,
survey answers, common questions, sales data, notes from
conversations]. Describe my two or three main customer groups: who
they are, what they want, what they worry about, why they choose
us, and what might make them leave. Then suggest one change for each
group that could make them buy more often.
```

```result
GROUP 1: WEEKDAY REGULARS (about 50% of sales)
Who: local workers and retirees; visit 2-4 times a week
Want: speed, consistency, a friendly hello
Worry: lines at 8 a.m.
One change: a "pre-order by text" option for the morning rush
GROUP 2: CELEBRATION CUSTOMERS...
```

**Make it better:** test the descriptions by asking five real customers whether they recognize themselves.

**Variations:**

- **Interview questions:** ask for ten questions to ask customers in a 15-minute chat.
- **Lost customers:** ask for a short survey for people who stopped buying.

## Workflow 93: Test a New Product or Service Idea

**Use it when:** you have an idea and want to test it before spending money on it.

> **Time Saved:** About 2 hours (from 2.5 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
I'm thinking about launching [idea] for [customers] at about [price].
Help me test it before I invest. Give me: 1) the strongest reasons
it could work; 2) the biggest reasons it could fail; 3) the key
assumptions I'm making; 4) three cheap tests I could run in the next
two weeks to check those assumptions, with what result would count
as a "yes"; 5) a rough break-even calculation, with your working.
```

```result
KEY ASSUMPTIONS
- Offices nearby want breakfast boxes at least weekly
- They'll pay $45 for a box for 8 people
CHEAP TESTS
1. Email 10 local offices with a sample menu; "yes" = 3 or more
   orders within two weeks
BREAK-EVEN: costs per box $24; margin $21; fixed costs $400 a month
-> 20 boxes a month to break even.
```

**Make it better:** ask Claude to play a skeptical customer and say why they wouldn't buy.

**Variations:**

- **Name ideas:** ask for ten names and check each yourself for existing trademarks.
- **Pricing test:** ask how to test two prices fairly.

## Workflow 94: Do a SWOT Analysis

**Use it when:** you want a clear-eyed view of your business before a big decision, a loan or a planning day.

> **Time Saved:** About 1 hour (from 75 minutes down to 15).

```
Do a SWOT analysis for [business]: strengths, weaknesses,
opportunities and threats. Use my business profile and these notes:
[recent results, competitors, changes in the area or industry]. Be
honest, specific and brief: five points per box at most. Then give
three actions: one that uses a strength to grab an opportunity, one
that fixes a weakness, and one that protects against a threat.
```

```result
STRENGTHS: loyal regulars; wholesale relationships; Sam's skill
WEAKNESSES: owner does too much; no online ordering
OPPORTUNITIES: new offices on River Road; gluten-free demand
THREATS: rising energy costs; a chain bakery opening nearby
ACTIONS:
1. Use wholesale relationships to pitch the River Road offices...
```

**Make it better:** ask your team to fill in the four boxes separately first, then paste all their answers in.

**Variations:**

- **For one product or service:** use the same prompt for a single part of the business.
- **Risk review:** ask for the top five risks, how likely each is, and one action each.

## Workflow 95: Write a One-Page Business Plan

**Use it when:** you need to set out where the business is going, for yourself, a partner or a lender.

> **Time Saved:** About 3 hours (from 3.5 hours down to 30 minutes) for a first draft.

```
Write a one-page business plan for [business]. Use my business
profile and these notes: vision [3-year vision], customers [who],
what we sell [products and prices], how we reach customers
[marketing], team [who], key numbers [sales, costs, targets],
milestones for the next 12 months [list], and what we need [money,
people, equipment]. Use short headings and bullet points.
```

```result
VISION: the best bakery in the county, and the first choice for
local cafés and offices.
CUSTOMERS: weekday regulars, celebration customers, wholesale cafés
12-MONTH MILESTONES
- Q1: hire a Saturday assistant
- Q2: launch office breakfast boxes
- Q3: online ordering live...
WHAT WE NEED: $15,000 for a second oven (see attached forecast)
```

> **Check First:** A lender will want figures it can check. Build the numbers with your accountant or in a spreadsheet (Workflow 54), not just from Claude.

**Make it better:** ask Claude to list the questions a lender or investor would ask, and fill the gaps.

**Variations:**

- **Full plan:** ask Claude to expand each section into a longer plan with a forecast appendix.
- **Elevator pitch:** ask for a 30-second spoken version.

## Workflow 96: Find New Ways to Grow

**Use it when:** sales have stalled and you want fresh ideas beyond "do more marketing".

> **Time Saved:** About 1.5 hours of brainstorming (from 2 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Suggest 15 practical ways to grow [business], a [description] with
[team size] and a budget of about [amount]. Group them: sell more to
current customers, find new customers, raise prices or margins, add
new products, and partnerships. For each: the idea in one line,
cost (low/medium/high), effort, and how soon it could pay off. Then
pick the top three for a business like mine and explain why.
```

```result
SELL MORE TO CURRENT CUSTOMERS
1. Loyalty card for coffee (low cost, low effort, 1 month)
2. Pre-order Sunday bread on Saturdays (low, low, 2 weeks)
PARTNERSHIPS
11. Bread for the farm shop on Saturdays (low, medium, 1 month)
TOP THREE: 2, 7 and 11, because they use what you already have...
```

**Make it better:** for each top idea, use Workflow 93 to test it cheaply first.

**Variations:**

- **Quiet season:** ask for ideas to fill the quietest months.
- **No budget:** ask for ten ideas that cost nothing but time.

## Workflow 97: Prepare a Pitch or Presentation

**Use it when:** you have to pitch to a big client, speak at an event or present to a bank.

> **Time Saved:** About 2 hours (from 2.5 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Help me prepare a [length]-minute presentation to [audience] about
[topic]. What I want them to do afterwards: [goal]. Key facts: [list].
Give me: a slide-by-slide outline (title and three points max per
slide), a speaker script for each slide in my voice, a strong
opening line and closing line, and the five hardest questions they
might ask, with short answers.
```

```result
SLIDE 1: "Fresh bread by 7 a.m., every day"
Script: "Good morning. Last year, our cafés threw away 30% less
bread than with their old suppliers. Here's how..."
SLIDE 2: Who we are (three bullet points)
...
HARD QUESTION: "What happens if your van breaks down?" -> "We have
a backup arrangement with a local courier, tested twice this year."
```

**Make it better:** practice aloud, then ask Claude to cut the script by 20%. Spoken presentations almost always run long.

**Variations:**

- **Award entry:** paste the entry criteria and ask for a draft that answers each one.
- **Trade show:** ask for a 30-second stand pitch and three opening questions.

## Workflow 98: Draft a Grant or Funding Application

**Use it when:** you have found a grant, award or competition and the application form is daunting.

> **Time Saved:** About 3 hours (from 4 hours down to 1 hour).

```
I'm applying for [grant name] from [funder]. Here are the criteria
and questions: [paste them]. About my business and project: [use my
business profile, plus project details, costs and expected results].
For each question: draft an answer within the word limit, using
the funder's own language where it fits, with specific numbers and
evidence. Mark anything I need to check or add as [ADD]. Then list
which criteria my answers are weakest on.
```

```result
Q3: "How will the project benefit the local community?" (max 200
words)
Draft: "The new oven will let us supply free day-old bread to the
Northbrook Food Bank five days a week, up from two..."
[ADD: number of families the food bank serves each week]
WEAKEST CRITERIA: "Environmental impact" - you haven't given figures
on energy savings from the new oven.
```

> **Check First:** Every fact in a funding application must be true and checkable. Read the funder's rules carefully, and never let Claude invent numbers or results.

**Make it better:** keep your best answers in your Project. Many grants ask similar questions, so the next application is faster.

**Variations:**

- **Finding grants:** ask Claude what kinds of grants or support might suit your business, then search official sources for current programs.
- **Award nomination:** use the same prompt for local business awards.

## Workflow 99: Plan Your Online Presence

**Use it when:** your website, listings and social profiles feel out of date, scattered or inconsistent.

> **Time Saved:** About 2 hours (from 2.5 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Review my online presence. Here's what I have: [website pages, text
from your Google Business Profile, social media bios, directory
listings]. Check for: inconsistent details (hours, address, phone,
prices), missing information customers need, weak or unclear
descriptions, and missing calls to action. Give me a prioritized
fix list, then rewrite my website home page intro and my business
profile description.
```

```result
INCONSISTENCIES
- Sunday hours: website says 8-1, Google says 9-2. Which is right?
- Phone number missing from Instagram bio.
FIX LIST (in order)
1. Make hours match everywhere (10 min)
2. Add "Order a cake" button to the home page (20 min)
3. Add 10 recent photos to your Google profile...
```

**Make it better:** ask for a checklist to repeat every quarter.

**Variations:**

- **New website:** ask for a page list and the text for each page.
- **Search visibility:** ask for the words customers might search for, and where to use them naturally on your site.

## Workflow 100: Write Your Annual Plan

**Use it when:** a new year is coming and you want a clear plan for it.

> **Time Saved:** About 3 hours (from 4 hours down to 1 hour).

```
Help me write next year's plan for [business]. This year: [results,
highlights, problems, and my monthly reviews if I have them]. What
I want next year: [goals for the business and for me personally].
Give me: a one-paragraph summary of this year; three to five goals
for next year with measures; a quarter-by-quarter plan; a simple
budget outline; what I'll stop doing; and the first five actions
for January.
```

```result
THIS YEAR IN ONE PARAGRAPH: sales grew 14% to $256,000, wholesale
reached 36%, but you worked six days a week all year...
GOALS FOR NEXT YEAR
1. Sales $290,000 (+13%)
2. Wholesale 40% of sales
3. Owner works five days a week by June
STOP DOING: the Sunday market (low profit, high effort)
FIRST FIVE ACTIONS FOR JANUARY: ...
```

**Make it better:** print the one-page version and put it on the wall, then use Workflow 90 every month to check progress.

**Variations:**

- **Team version:** ask for a short, inspiring version to share with staff.
- **Personal plan:** ask for a plan for your own skills, health and time off alongside the business plan.
