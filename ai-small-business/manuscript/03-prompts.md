# Chapter 3: How to Use the Workflows

Every prompt in Part II is ready to copy. This short chapter explains how they are built, so you can fill them in quickly, fix them when the result is not quite right, and write your own.

## The Five Parts of a Good Business Prompt

Almost every workflow prompt has the same five parts. Once you see them, you will notice them everywhere:

1. **Role and context:** who you are and what the business is. ("I run a family bakery...") If you have a Project with a business profile, this part is already done.
2. **The task:** exactly what you want. ("Write a reply to this customer complaint.")
3. **The input:** the material to work with, pasted after the instructions. (The customer's email, your notes, the figures.)
4. **The rules:** tone, length, things to include or avoid. ("Under 150 words. Apologize once, not three times. Offer a replacement, not a refund.")
5. **The format:** how the result should look. ("Give me the email only, with a subject line.")

Here is a short prompt with all five:

```
I run a family bakery. Please write a reply to the customer email
below. Be warm and apologize once. Offer to remake the cake free of
charge this weekend, but do not offer a refund. Keep it under 120
words and give me a subject line.

[paste the customer's email here]
```

## Filling In the Brackets

Words in square brackets are placeholders. Replace each one, including the brackets, with your own details:

- `[type of business]` becomes *a family bakery*
- `[paste the customer's email here]` becomes the actual email

If a bracket does not apply to you, delete it. If you are unsure what to put, describe your situation in plain words; Claude copes well with messy input.

## The Three-Round Habit

The first answer is a draft. The best results usually come from two or three short follow-ups, as you would give a new assistant:

- "Shorter, and less formal."
- "Good. Now add a line about our Saturday opening hours."
- "Give me three other options for the first sentence."
- "That's too salesy. Make it sound like a neighbor, not an advert."

Do not start a new chat to fix a draft. Keep talking in the same one, because Claude remembers what you have already said in that conversation.

## Pasting Material In

Many workflows say "paste your notes" or "paste the email". You can:

- **Copy and paste text** into the message box.
- **Attach a file**: a PDF, a spreadsheet, a Word document or a photo. Look for a paperclip or plus button next to the message box.
- **Take a photo** with the phone app: a receipt, a whiteboard, a handwritten list.

Before you paste, remove anything private that Claude does not need, such as bank details or customers' home addresses. Chapter 4 explains why.

## Reading the Example Results

Each workflow shows an **example result** for a fictional business. These are shortened, so you can see the shape of the answer without pages of text. Your result will differ, because it will be about your business, and because Claude rarely gives exactly the same answer twice. That is normal.

## The Time-Saved Notes

Each workflow has a **Time Saved** note, such as "about 40 minutes (from 50 down to 10)". These are estimates for a typical small business doing the job once, after the first try. Treat them as a rough guide to where the biggest wins are, not as a guarantee.

## Saving Your Favorites

As you try workflows, you will find a handful you use every week. Save them somewhere you can reach in seconds:

- In your Claude Project's files, as a document called *My workflows*.
- In a note on your phone or computer.
- In the bonus prompt library, with your own details already filled in.

Chapter 15 shows how to turn your favorites into a proper toolkit for you and your team.

## Key Points

- Good prompts have five parts: context, task, input, rules and format.
- Replace every bracket with your own details.
- Improve the draft with two or three short follow-ups in the same chat.
- Results vary; the examples show the shape, not the exact words.
