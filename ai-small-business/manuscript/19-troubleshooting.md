# Chapter 19: When the Result Isn't Right

Sometimes a workflow gives you something that's nearly right, or not right at all. That's normal. A first draft from Claude is like a first draft from a new member of staff: useful, but you'll usually want to adjust it. This chapter lists the most common problems business owners run into and the quickest fix for each.

The general rule: **don't start again; say what's wrong.** A short, specific note ("too formal, and cut it to 80 words") fixes most drafts in one round.

## "It Doesn't Sound Like Me"

**What you see:** the draft is polished but generic, with phrases you would never use, like "I hope this email finds you well" or "elevate your experience".

**Quick fixes:**

- Paste two or three things you've written yourself and say: "Rewrite this in the style of these examples."
- Add the words you never use to your Brand Voice Guide and your Project (Chapter 2).
- Ask for plainer language: "Use short sentences and everyday words. No marketing language."

```
This doesn't sound like me. I'm [describe how you talk: direct,
friendly, a bit dry]. Remove any phrase a big company would use,
cut the length by a third, and use words a customer would say out
loud.
```

## "It Made Something Up"

**What you see:** a price, date, policy, statistic or quote that isn't true. This is the most important problem to catch, because it can mislead customers.

**Quick fixes:**

- Give Claude the facts. Most made-up details fill a gap where information was missing. Your business profile, FAQ sheet and price list prevent most of them.
- Add this instruction to the prompt or your Project: "Use only the facts I give you. If something is missing, write [CHECK] instead of guessing."
- For research, ask: "Which of these points are you sure of, and which should I check?" If your plan includes web search, ask for sources and click through to them.

> **Warning:** Never publish a statistic, review, testimonial, legal statement or health claim that you haven't checked yourself. The responsibility is yours, not the software's.

## "It's Too Long"

**What you see:** you asked for an email and got an essay.

**Quick fixes:**

- Put a number in the prompt: "under 100 words", "five bullet points", "three sentences".
- After the draft, say: "Half the length. Keep the facts and the request."
- For social posts and texts, give the character limit.

## "It's Too Vague"

**What you see:** advice like "consider running a promotion" or "engage with your community", which you could have written yourself.

**Quick fixes:**

- Give more detail about your business: the town, the customers, the budget, the season, what you've already tried.
- Ask for specifics: "Give me three ideas I could start this week, each with a cost and the exact words to use."
- Ask Claude to ask you questions first: "Before you answer, ask me five questions about my business."

## "It Missed Part of What I Asked"

**What you see:** you asked for four things and got three.

**Quick fixes:**

- Number the parts of your request (the workflows do this for you).
- Reply: "You missed part 3. Please add it."
- For long requests, split them: ask for the plan first, then the emails, then the posts.

## "The Numbers Are Wrong"

**What you see:** a total that doesn't add up, a percentage that looks odd, a forecast that drifts.

**Quick fixes:**

- Ask Claude to "show your working step by step", so you can see where it went wrong.
- Ask for spreadsheet formulas instead of finished figures (Workflow 60), and let the spreadsheet do the arithmetic.
- Check every figure that will reach a customer, an accountant, a bank or a tax form.

## "It Refused or Hedged"

**What you see:** Claude won't do something, or buries the answer in caveats.

**Quick fixes:**

- Explain the legitimate business reason. "I'm the owner and I need to write a firm but fair final notice for an unpaid invoice" works better than "write a threatening letter".
- If you want fewer caveats, say so: "I understand this isn't legal advice and I'll check it with my lawyer. Please give me the draft."
- Some requests are refused for good reasons, such as writing fake reviews or pretending to be someone else. Those are worth avoiding anyway (Chapter 4).

## "It Forgot What We Agreed Earlier"

**What you see:** in a long chat, Claude stops following an instruction you gave at the start.

**Quick fixes:**

- Start a new chat for each new job. Long chats that wander between topics give weaker results.
- Put lasting instructions in your Project, not in the chat, so they apply every time.
- Repeat the key rule: "Remember: no discounts over 10%."

## "It Can't Read My File"

**What you see:** Claude says it can't open a file, or gets details wrong from a scan or photo.

**Quick fixes:**

- Check the file type and size; common types such as PDF, Word, Excel, CSV and images work best.
- For photos, use good light, hold the phone straight above the page and crop out the background.
- For spreadsheets, remove extra tabs and give the columns clear headings.
- If a scan is blurry, type the key figures yourself.

## "I've Hit My Usage Limit"

**What you see:** a message saying you've reached the limit for now.

**Quick fixes:**

- Batch your work: ask for five replies in one message instead of five separate chats.
- Use new chats rather than one very long one; long conversations use more of your allowance.
- If you hit the limit often, compare the plans on the Claude website. For many businesses, the time saved pays for a higher plan quickly.

## A Five-Second Checklist Before Anything Goes Out

Before you send, post or print anything Claude helped with, check:

1. **Facts:** are names, prices, dates and policies right?
2. **Promises:** does it offer anything you can't or won't deliver?
3. **Tone:** would you be happy saying this to the customer's face?
4. **Privacy:** does it include anything personal it shouldn't?
5. **Sense:** does it actually answer what was asked?

> **Remember:** Claude drafts, you decide. Every workflow in this book is designed to save you the blank page, not to take away your judgment.
