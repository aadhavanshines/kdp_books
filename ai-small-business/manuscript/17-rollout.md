# Chapter 17: Your 30-Day Plan

Reading about workflows is easy. Changing how you work is harder. This plan spreads the change over four weeks, about 20 to 30 minutes a day, so the habit builds without disrupting the business. By day 30 you'll have a working toolkit, a few hours back each week, and a clear idea of what to do next.

Tick off each day as you go. If you miss a day, just carry on; this isn't a test.

## Measuring What You've Saved

Each workflow in this book has a **Time Saved** note. They're estimates; your real savings depend on your business, how fast you type and how much you edit. The best way to know is to measure.

Keep a simple tally for the 30 days. Each time you use a workflow, write down the workflow number and roughly how many minutes it saved compared with doing it the old way. At the end of the month, add it up.

```
At the end of each week, here are the workflows I used and the
minutes I think each saved: [list]. Add them up, tell me which saved
the most, and suggest two workflows from my list of annoying tasks
that I should try next week.
```

Many owners find the real benefit isn't just the hours. It's the jobs that finally get done: the procedure that's been in your head for years, the price increase you kept avoiding, the review replies that make customers feel heard.

## After the 30 Days

You've built the habit. From here:

- **Keep the monthly rhythm.** Do your monthly review (Workflow 90) on the first of every month, and spend ten minutes keeping your Projects up to date (Chapter 15).
- **Add one new workflow a month.** Choose one from your list of annoying tasks.
- **Teach your team.** The more people use the same Projects and prompts, the more consistent your business becomes.
- **Write your own workflows.** Once you've used a few dozen, you'll know the pattern: say who Claude should be, give the facts, describe the result and the format, then check and refine (Chapter 3). The best workflows for your business are the ones you write yourself.
