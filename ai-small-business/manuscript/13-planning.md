# Chapter 13: Meetings, Planning and Productivity

Running a small business means doing ten jobs at once, often without a plan. These workflows help you plan your week, run shorter meetings, follow up properly and make better decisions. Several of them work best as regular habits: the weekly plan on Monday, the follow-up after every meeting, the monthly review on the first of the month.

## Workflow 81: Plan Your Day

**Use it when:** you start the day with a long list and no idea what to do first.

> **Time Saved:** About 20 minutes a day (from 25 down to 5), and fewer forgotten jobs.

```
Here's everything on my mind today: [brain dump, in any order].
Fixed appointments: [times]. I have about [hours] of working time.
Sort my list into: must do today, should do today, can wait, and
can delegate (and to whom). Then give me a realistic schedule for
today with breaks, putting the hardest task when I have the most
energy, which is [morning / afternoon].
```

```result
MUST DO TODAY
- Confirm Saturday's wedding cake order (10 min)
- Pay Mill Flour Co. invoice (5 min)
SCHEDULE
7:00-8:30 Bake (as usual)
9:00-9:45 Price review for the café contract (hardest; do first)
9:45-10:00 Pay invoice, confirm cake order
...
DELEGATE: "Order more boxes" -> Leah
```

**Make it better:** use your phone's voice input to do the brain dump while you walk or drive.

**Variations:**

- **End-of-day version:** ask "What did I finish, what moves to tomorrow, and what should I drop?"
- **Overwhelmed day:** ask for "the three things that matter most if I only get three done".

## Workflow 82: Plan Your Week

**Use it when:** weeks go by without the important, non-urgent work getting done.

> **Time Saved:** About 40 minutes a week (from 50 down to 10).

```
Help me plan next week. My goals this month: [goals]. Fixed
commitments: [meetings, deliveries, appointments]. Ongoing work:
[list]. New tasks: [list]. Plan the week day by day, with no more
than three main tasks per day, protect one block of time for
[important but not urgent work], and list anything I should say no
to or move to next week.
```

```result
GOAL FOCUS THIS WEEK: sign the Copper Pot contract; launch the
Christmas order form.
MONDAY: write the Christmas order form (2 hours, protected)...
TUESDAY: follow up with Priya; review the cake price list...
SAY NO TO / MOVE: the trade fair stall (not this year); the new
logo idea (January).
```

**Make it better:** use the bonus **Weekly Planner** template, and paste last week's plan with notes on what actually happened, so Claude can plan more realistically.

**Variations:**

- **Team week:** ask for a plan that assigns tasks to each team member.
- **Quiet season:** ask for a week focused on improvement projects, not daily work.

## Workflow 83: Write a Meeting Agenda

**Use it when:** your meetings run long and end without clear decisions.

> **Time Saved:** About 15 minutes per meeting to write, and often 20 minutes off the meeting itself.

```
Write an agenda for a [length] meeting with [who] about [topic]. What
we need to decide or achieve: [goals]. Put the decisions first,
give each item a time and an owner, add one line saying what each
item needs to produce (a decision, a date, a name), and end with
5 minutes for actions and owners.
```

```result
TEAM MEETING - TUESDAY, 3:15 P.M. (30 MINUTES)
1. Christmas opening hours (10 min, Sam) -> DECISION: final hours
2. Saturday staffing (10 min, Leah) -> DECISION: who covers Dec 21
3. New allergen labels (5 min, Maria) -> UPDATE
4. Actions and owners (5 min)
```

**Make it better:** send the agenda the day before, so people arrive ready to decide.

**Variations:**

- **Client meeting:** ask for an agenda that ends with an agreed next step.
- **Supplier review:** ask for an agenda covering performance, prices and problems.

## Workflow 84: Turn Meeting Notes Into a Follow-Up Email

**Use it when:** you've just left a meeting or call, and you want everyone to agree on what was decided.

> **Time Saved:** About 25 minutes per meeting (from 30 down to 5).

```
Here are my rough notes from a meeting with [who] about [topic].
Turn them into a short follow-up email: thank them, list what we
agreed, list actions with owners and dates, and note any open
questions. Under 200 words. Don't add anything that isn't in my
notes; if something's unclear, mark it [CHECK].

[paste notes or a transcript]
```

```result
Subject: Thanks for today - next steps on the café deliveries

Hi Priya,

Thanks for your time this morning. Here's what we agreed:
- Two-week trial starting Monday, March 4, both sites
- Delivery by 7 a.m., Tuesday to Sunday
ACTIONS
- Sam: send the trial price list by Friday
- Priya: confirm the delivery entrance at the Elm Road site
OPEN QUESTION: [CHECK: did we agree who pays for returned loaves?]
```

**Make it better:** send it within an hour of the meeting, while everyone still remembers.

> **Check First:** If you record a meeting to get a transcript, tell everyone first and follow the recording rules where you are.

**Variations:**

- **Internal meeting:** ask for a short action list for the team chat.
- **Phone call:** use the same prompt with the notes you jotted during the call.

## Workflow 85: Make a Decision

**Use it when:** you're stuck between options and keep going round in circles.

> **Time Saved:** About 1 hour of going round in circles (from 75 minutes down to 15).

```
Help me decide [decision]. Options: [list]. What matters most to me:
[criteria, in order of importance]. What I know about each option:
[facts]. Make a simple comparison table, scoring each option on
each criterion, explain the trade-offs, tell me what you'd want to
know before deciding, and ask me three questions that might change
the answer. Don't decide for me.
```

```result
| | Second shop | Bigger kitchen | Stay as we are |
| Profit potential | High | Medium | Low |
| Risk | High | Medium | Low |
| My time | Much more | A bit more | Same |
TRADE-OFF: a second shop could double sales, but you'd spend less
time baking, which you said you love most.
QUESTIONS: Who would run the second shop day to day?
```

**Make it better:** ask Claude to argue for the option you're leaning against. It often reveals what you really think.

**Variations:**

- **Pre-mortem:** ask "Imagine it's a year later and this decision failed. What went wrong?"
- **Quick choice:** ask for a one-paragraph recommendation for small, reversible decisions.

## Workflow 86: Set Goals for the Next Quarter

**Use it when:** you want to make the next three months count instead of just getting through them.

> **Time Saved:** About 1.5 hours (from 2 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Help me set goals for the next quarter for [business]. Where we are:
[key numbers, recent wins and problems]. What I want in a year:
[vision]. Suggest three goals for the next 90 days, each with a
clear measure, a target, the first three actions, and what could
get in the way. Keep it realistic for a business with [team size]
and [hours] I can spend on it.
```

```result
GOAL 1: Grow wholesale to 40% of sales (now 35%)
Measure: wholesale sales as % of total, checked monthly
First actions: 1. Approach 5 cafés (Workflow 21); 2. Make a
wholesale price list; 3. Offer two-week trials
What could get in the way: early-morning delivery capacity...
```

**Make it better:** check your goals every month with Workflow 90.

**Variations:**

- **Personal goals:** ask for goals for your own time, such as taking one full day off a week.
- **Team goals:** ask for one goal per team member that supports the business goals.

## Workflow 87: Delegate a Task Clearly

**Use it when:** you want to hand something over, but you end up doing it yourself because explaining takes too long.

> **Time Saved:** About 20 minutes per handover, and hours of your time each month.

```
I want to hand over [task] to [role]. Here's how I do it now: [rough
description]. Write a clear handover brief: what the task is and why
it matters, what "done well" looks like, the steps, what they can
decide alone and what they should check with me, the deadline, and
where to find what they need.
```

```result
TASK: weekly ordering of packaging
WHY IT MATTERS: running out of cake boxes on a Saturday costs us
sales.
DONE WELL: we never drop below two weeks of stock; orders are
placed by Tuesday noon.
YOU CAN DECIDE: quantities up to $300.
CHECK WITH SAM: anything over $300, or a new supplier.
```

**Make it better:** ask the person to read the brief and tell you what's unclear before they start.

**Variations:**

- **To a freelancer:** ask for a brief with the budget, deadline and how you'll give feedback.
- **To Claude itself:** for repeated tasks, turn the brief into a saved prompt in your Project (Chapter 15).

## Workflow 88: Get Through Your Reading Pile

**Use it when:** industry newsletters, reports and articles pile up unread.

> **Time Saved:** About 1 hour a week (from 75 minutes down to 15).

```
Here are [number] articles and newsletters I haven't had time to
read. For each, give me a two-line summary and a rating: "read in
full", "skim", or "skip", for the owner of a [business type]. Then
list any ideas I could actually use this month.

[paste text or attach files]
```

```result
1. "Energy costs for small food businesses" - two-line summary...
   Rating: read in full (your biggest rising cost)
2. "10 TikTok trends for spring" - skip (not your audience)
IDEAS TO USE:
- Ask your energy supplier about a fixed-rate contract before
  March.
```

**Make it better:** ask Claude to explain anything technical in plain English.

**Variations:**

- **Podcast or video:** paste a transcript and ask for the key points.
- **Book notes:** paste your highlights and ask for five actions for your business.

## Workflow 89: Create a Simple Project Plan

**Use it when:** you have a bigger project, such as a refit, a new product or a website, with many moving parts.

> **Time Saved:** About 1.5 hours (from 2 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Help me plan [project]. Goal: [what done looks like]. Deadline:
[date]. Budget: [amount]. People involved: [names, roles]. Give me:
the main stages, the tasks in each stage with owners and dates, the
order things must happen in, the three biggest risks with a plan
for each, and a weekly checklist I can use to track progress.
```

```result
PROJECT: Christmas order form and collection system (deadline:
Nov 15)
STAGE 1: Decide the menu and prices (Sam, by Oct 20)
STAGE 2: Build the online form (Leah, by Nov 1)
STAGE 3: Test with 5 regular customers (by Nov 8)
RISKS: the form takes too many orders for one day -> set a daily
limit...
```

**Make it better:** paste the weekly checklist with progress notes each week and ask "Are we on track? What should I do this week?"

**Variations:**

- **Renovation:** ask for a plan that keeps the business trading during the work.
- **New product launch:** combine with Workflows 3 and 6 for the marketing side.

## Workflow 90: Do a Monthly Business Review

**Use it when:** the first of the month comes around, and you want to learn from last month rather than just survive the next.

> **Time Saved:** About 1.5 hours (from 2 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Help me review last month for [business]. Numbers: [sales, costs,
customers, other key numbers, with the month before]. What went
well: [list]. What went badly: [list]. Last month's goals and
progress: [list]. Give me: a short summary, three lessons, a check
on my quarterly goals, and the three priorities for this month.
Ask me two questions I should think about.
```

```result
SUMMARY: a strong month for sales (+8%), but two late deliveries and
a staff shortage on Saturdays.
LESSONS: 1. Saturday is now the busiest day; staff it like one...
GOALS CHECK: wholesale growth on track (37%, target 40%)...
PRIORITIES THIS MONTH: 1. Hire a Saturday assistant (Workflow 61)...
QUESTIONS: What would you stop doing if you had to cut five hours a
week?
```

**Make it better:** use the bonus **Monthly Review** template and keep every review in your Project, so Claude can spot patterns over the year.

**Variations:**

- **Annual review:** paste your twelve monthly reviews and ask for the year's story and next year's priorities.
- **Team review:** ask for a version to share with staff, without private figures.
