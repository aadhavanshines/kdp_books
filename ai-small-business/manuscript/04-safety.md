# Chapter 4: Ground Rules: Accuracy, Privacy and Your Reputation

Using AI in a business is not risky if you follow a few simple rules. Ignoring them can cost you money, a customer or your reputation. Read this chapter once, and share it with anyone on your team who uses Claude.

## Rule 1: Claude Drafts, You Decide

Read everything before it goes out. Every email, post, policy and quote. Claude writes fluently, which makes mistakes easy to miss. Check especially:

- **Names, dates, prices and numbers.** Claude sometimes changes or invents them.
- **Promises.** A friendly draft may offer a refund, a discount or a delivery date you never agreed to.
- **Facts and claims.** "Voted best in the county" is a problem if you never were.

## Rule 2: Check Anything About Money, Law, Tax or People

Claude can explain, summarize and organize. It is not a lawyer, accountant, tax adviser or HR professional, and it does not know the rules in your country, state or industry with certainty. When a workflow touches these areas, it carries a **Check First** box. Use Claude to prepare, and a professional or an official source to confirm.

## Rule 3: Keep Private Information Private

What you type into Claude is sent to Anthropic's servers to produce an answer. Well-known AI companies take security seriously, but the safest habit is to share only what the job needs.

**Never paste:**

- Passwords, PINs or sign-in codes
- Full card numbers or bank account details
- Social Security, tax ID or passport numbers
- Customers' or employees' health information

**Think twice before pasting:**

- Customers' full names, home addresses and phone numbers. You can usually replace them with "the customer" or initials.
- Employee performance details. Describe the situation without names where you can.
- Confidential contracts or supplier pricing. Check your agreements first.

Look at your plan's privacy settings (Chapter 2) and decide whether your chats may be used to improve the service. Business and team plans usually offer stronger privacy commitments; check the current terms for your plan.

## Rule 4: Be Honest With Customers

- Do not publish fake reviews, testimonials or "customer stories". Many countries treat them as illegal deceptive advertising.
- Do not let an AI pretend to be a named human in a live chat with customers.
- If you use AI to help write customer replies, that is fine; you are still responsible for what they say.

## Rule 5: Respect Other People's Work

- Do not ask Claude to copy a competitor's website, menu descriptions or marketing word for word.
- Images and logos: Claude can describe ideas for them, but check the licensing of any image you publish.
- Claim only what is true about your own business.

## Rule 6: Keep a Human in the Loop for Anything Automatic

Part III shows how to connect Claude to your email and other tools. Start every automation in **draft mode**, where Claude prepares the message and a person presses send. Move to full automation only for low-risk jobs, once you trust the results.

## A One-Page Policy for Your Team

If others in your business use Claude, give them a short written policy. This prompt writes a first draft:

```
Please write a one-page AI use policy for my small business, [type of
business], with [number] staff. Cover: what staff may use AI for,
what they must never paste into it (customer and payment details,
passwords, health information), the rule that a person must review
everything before it is sent or published, honesty with customers,
and who to ask when unsure. Plain English, friendly tone, bullet
points.
```

> **Check First:** If you handle sensitive data, such as health, legal or financial records, or you work in a regulated industry, ask your professional adviser what your rules allow before using any AI tool with that data.

## Key Points

- Read everything before it goes out, especially numbers and promises.
- Use professionals for money, law, tax and HR decisions.
- Share only what the job needs; never passwords, card or ID numbers.
- No fake reviews, no pretending AI is a person.
- Start automations in draft mode.
