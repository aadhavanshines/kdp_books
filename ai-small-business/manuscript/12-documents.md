# Chapter 12: Proposals, Quotes and Documents

Every small business writes documents that win or lose work: proposals, quotes, case studies, terms, bios and reports. These workflows give you a strong first draft in minutes, built from your own facts.

> **Check First:** Contracts, terms and conditions and anything with legal weight should be reviewed by a qualified professional before you rely on them. Claude can help you understand and draft; it is not a lawyer.

## Workflow 71: Write a Business Proposal

**Use it when:** a potential client has asked you to "put something in writing".

> **Time Saved:** About 2 hours (from 2.5 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Write a proposal from [business] to [client] for [project]. Their
situation and goals: [what they told you]. What we'll do: [scope].
Timeline: [dates]. Price: [price and payment terms]. Why us: [real
reasons, experience, proof]. Structure: a one-paragraph summary,
their goals, our approach, what's included and not included,
timeline, price, next steps. Plain English, confident but not
pushy, under 900 words. Mark any place where I need to add a fact.
```

```result
SUMMARY
Copper Pot Café wants fresh bread and pastries delivered daily to
both sites by 7 a.m., at a price that keeps food costs under 30%.
We propose a two-week trial, then a rolling monthly agreement...
WHAT'S INCLUDED
- Daily delivery, Tuesday to Sunday, to both sites by 7 a.m.
- A fixed menu of six breads and four pastries, changed seasonally
NOT INCLUDED: sandwiches or cakes (available separately)
[ADD: a quote from one of our current café customers]
```

**Make it better:** paste your notes from the first meeting so the proposal uses the client's own words for their goals.

**Variations:**

- **One-page version:** ask for a proposal that fits on a single page for smaller jobs.
- **Response to a tender:** paste the requirements and ask for a draft that answers each one in order.

## Workflow 72: Write a Detailed Quote

**Use it when:** you need to turn a job into clear line items a customer will understand.

> **Time Saved:** About 40 minutes (from 50 down to 10).

```
Turn these job notes into a clear quote. Job: [description]. Labor:
[hours and rate]. Materials: [items and costs]. Other costs: [travel,
disposal, hire]. Tax: [rate, if any]. Show each line with quantity,
unit price and total, then subtotal, tax and total. Add: what's
included, what's not, how long the quote is valid, and payment
terms. Show the arithmetic so I can check it.
```

```result
| Item | Qty | Unit | Total |
| Labor (2 people, 8 hours) | 16 | $45.00 | $720.00 |
| Copper pipe, 15 mm | 12 m | $6.50 | $78.00 |
| Disposal of old boiler | 1 | $85.00 | $85.00 |
Subtotal: $883.00   Tax (8%): $70.64   Total: $953.64
Valid for 30 days. 50% deposit to book; balance on completion.
```

> **Check First:** Check every quantity, price and total before sending. Claude can make arithmetic slips.

**Make it better:** once you have a good quote, save it in your Project as the model for future ones.

**Variations:**

- **Good, better, best:** ask for three versions at different levels of work.
- **Estimate, not quote:** ask for a version with a price range and the reasons it might change.

## Workflow 73: Write a Case Study

**Use it when:** you did a great job for a customer and want to use it to win more work.

> **Time Saved:** About 1.5 hours (from 2 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Write a short case study about our work for [customer, with their
permission]. Their problem: [problem]. What we did: [work]. Results:
[real results, with numbers where possible]. A quote from them:
[their words, if you have them]. Structure: headline, the challenge,
what we did, the results, the customer's words. Under 400 words.
Don't add results I haven't given you.
```

```result
HEADLINE: How Copper Pot Café cut bread waste by a third
THE CHALLENGE: Priya's two cafés were throwing away up to 20 loaves
a week from their old supplier...
THE RESULTS: in the first three months, waste fell from 20 loaves to
13 a week...
"The bread lasts longer, and the 7 a.m. delivery has never been
late." - Priya, owner
```

> **Check First:** Get the customer's written permission before publishing their name, words or results.

**Make it better:** ask the customer three questions by email first (Workflow 25's referral email is a good moment), then paste their answers in.

**Variations:**

- **Social post version:** ask for a 100-word version with one striking number.
- **Before and after:** ask for a short "before / after" summary for your website.

## Workflow 74: Draft Simple Terms and Conditions

**Use it when:** you have no written terms, and misunderstandings keep costing you.

> **Time Saved:** About 2 hours on the first draft (from 2.5 hours down to 30 minutes), before your professional check.

```
Help me draft plain-English terms and conditions for [business] in
[country / state]. We provide [service / products]. Key points I
want covered: [booking and deposits, cancellations, payment terms,
late payment, what happens if we're delayed, liability, complaints].
My rules: [your rules]. Use short sections with headings. Then list
the points a lawyer should check, and anything that might conflict
with consumer law.
```

```result
1. BOOKINGS AND DEPOSITS
A booking is confirmed when we receive a 25% deposit...
2. CANCELLATIONS
If you cancel more than 14 days before the start date, we'll refund
your deposit in full...
FOR YOUR LAWYER TO CHECK:
- Whether a non-refundable deposit is allowed where you trade
- The wording of the liability section
```

> **Check First:** Terms that are unfair or unclear may not be enforceable. Have a lawyer review them before use.

**Make it better:** ask Claude for a short "key terms" box to show customers before they book.

**Variations:**

- **Website terms:** ask for terms of use for your website (then have them checked).
- **Supplier terms:** ask Claude to explain a supplier's terms to you in plain English and point out anything unusual.

## Workflow 75: Understand a Contract Before You Sign

**Use it when:** someone has sent you a contract, lease or agreement and it's long, dense and important.

> **Time Saved:** About 1 hour (from 75 minutes down to 15), and better questions for your lawyer.

```
I've been sent the agreement below. Explain it in plain English:
1) a one-paragraph summary of what I'm agreeing to; 2) my main
obligations and costs; 3) how and when I can end it; 4) anything
unusual, one-sided or risky, with the clause number; 5) questions I
should ask before signing. Don't tell me whether to sign.

[paste or attach the agreement, with personal details removed]
```

```result
SUMMARY: a five-year lease of the shop at a rent of $2,400 a month,
rising each year by the inflation rate...
RISKY OR UNUSUAL:
- Clause 14.2: you pay for all repairs to the roof and walls, not
  just the inside. This could be expensive in an older building.
- Clause 19: the landlord can end the lease with 3 months' notice;
  you can't end it early at all.
QUESTIONS TO ASK: Can we add a break clause after year three?
```

> **Check First:** Use this to prepare, not to decide. For anything significant, have a lawyer review the contract before you sign.

**Make it better:** ask Claude to turn the "questions to ask" into a polite email to the other party.

**Variations:**

- **Compare two versions:** paste the old and new versions and ask what changed.
- **Software subscriptions:** ask what happens to your data if you cancel.

## Workflow 76: Write Your About Page and Bios

**Use it when:** you need a business story, an owner bio or team bios for a website, award entry or local paper.

> **Time Saved:** About 1 hour (from 75 minutes down to 15).

```
Write an About page for [business]. The story: [how it started, why,
key moments]. What we believe: [values]. Who we serve: [customers].
Facts: [years, location, team, milestones]. Then write: a 50-word
owner bio in the third person, a 25-word version, and a 2-sentence
version for social media. Warm, specific, no clichés like
"passionate" or "one-stop shop".
```

```result
ABOUT US
In 2016, Sam Okafor was baking bread in a rented kitchen at 3 a.m.
and selling it from a folding table on Mill Street. Eight years
later, the folding table is a shop...
50-WORD BIO:
Sam Okafor founded Northbrook Bakery in 2016 after ten years as a
hotel pastry chef...
```

**Make it better:** answer Claude's questions in an interview first: "Ask me ten questions about the business's story, one at a time."

**Variations:**

- **Team page:** paste short notes on each person and ask for matching bios.
- **Speaker bio:** ask for a version to be read aloud when you're introduced at an event.

## Workflow 77: Write a Company Profile or Capability Statement

**Use it when:** a bigger customer, local government office or corporate buyer asks for "a short overview of your company".

> **Time Saved:** About 1.5 hours (from 2 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Write a two-page capability statement for [business]. Use my business
profile. Include: who we are, what we do (services), who we've
worked for (with permission), what makes us different, our
certifications and insurance, our capacity (team size, area,
volumes), and contact details. Professional and confident. Then
write a one-paragraph version for supplier forms.
```

```result
NORTHBROOK BAKERY - CAPABILITY STATEMENT
WHAT WE DO: daily wholesale bread and pastries for cafés, offices
and events within 15 miles of Northbrook...
CAPACITY: up to 800 loaves and 1,200 pastries a day; deliveries by
7 a.m., six days a week...
CERTIFICATIONS: [ADD: food hygiene rating and certificate numbers]
```

**Make it better:** keep the finished statement in your Project, so every tender or supplier form starts from it.

**Variations:**

- **Sector-specific:** ask for a version tailored to schools, offices or events.
- **Pitch slide:** ask for the same content as five short slides.

## Workflow 78: Turn a Document Into a Summary

**Use it when:** you have a long report, guide or regulation and need the key points fast.

> **Time Saved:** About 40 minutes per document (from 50 down to 10).

```
Summarize the document below for the owner of a small [type of
business]. Give me: 1) the five most important points in plain
English; 2) what, if anything, I need to do and by when; 3) anything
that might cost me money or put me at risk; 4) terms I might not
understand, explained. Quote the section numbers you rely on.

[paste or attach the document]
```

```result
WHAT YOU NEED TO DO:
- By April 1: display the new allergen information on your menus
  (section 3.2).
- Train staff on the new rules and keep a record (section 5.1).
TERMS EXPLAINED:
"Prepacked for direct sale": food packed on the premises before a
customer orders it, like your sandwiches in the cooler.
```

> **Check First:** For regulations, check the summary against the official source. Rules change, and details matter.

**Make it better:** ask follow-up questions, such as "Does this apply to market stalls?"

**Variations:**

- **For your team:** ask for a version staff can read in two minutes.
- **Compare with what we do now:** paste your current process and ask what needs to change.

## Workflow 79: Write a Report for a Bank, Landlord or Partner

**Use it when:** someone outside the business needs an update on how you're doing.

> **Time Saved:** About 1.5 hours (from 2 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Write a short business update for [bank / landlord / investor /
partner]. Period: [period]. Key figures: [sales, profit, customers,
other numbers]. What went well: [list]. Challenges and what we're
doing about them: [list]. Plans for the next [period]: [list]. What
we're asking for, if anything: [request]. Professional, honest and
concise, under 600 words with headings.
```

```result
NORTHBROOK BAKERY - UPDATE FOR [BANK], JANUARY TO JUNE
HEADLINES: sales up 14% to $128,000; wholesale now 35% of
revenue...
CHALLENGES: ingredient costs rose 11%. We raised loaf prices by
50 cents in April and are trialing a new flour supplier...
OUR REQUEST: we'd like to discuss extending the overdraft by $5,000
to cover the December peak.
```

**Make it better:** ask Claude "What questions will the bank manager ask after reading this?" and prepare the answers.

**Variations:**

- **Loan application:** ask for a draft business case for a specific loan (check every figure).
- **Annual letter:** ask for a letter to regular customers about the year.

## Workflow 80: Proofread and Improve Any Document

**Use it when:** a document matters and you want it clear, correct and professional before it goes out.

> **Time Saved:** About 20 minutes per document (from 30 down to 10).

```
Proofread the document below. First list: spelling and grammar
errors, unclear sentences, inconsistencies (names, dates, prices),
and anything a reader might misunderstand. Then give me an improved
version that keeps my meaning and voice. Don't add new facts. Keep
it about the same length.

[paste the document]
```

```result
ISSUES:
1. Paragraph 2: "it's" should be "its".
2. The delivery day is "Tuesday" in paragraph 1 and "Wednesday" in
   paragraph 4. Which is right?
3. "Asap" - spell out "as soon as possible" in a formal letter.
IMPROVED VERSION: ...
```

**Make it better:** ask for the changes as a list only, if you prefer to edit the document yourself.

**Variations:**

- **Tone check:** ask "Does this sound friendly, professional or cold? How would you make it warmer?"
- **Make it shorter:** ask for a version 40% shorter that keeps every fact.
