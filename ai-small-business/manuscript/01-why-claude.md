# Chapter 1: What Claude Can Do for a Small Business

Most small business owners do three jobs at once: the work itself, selling the work, and running the business around it. Claude can help a lot with the second and third, and a little with the first. This chapter sets expectations, so you know where Claude will save you hours and where it will not.

## Where Claude Saves the Most Time

**Writing anything you have written before.** Emails, replies, product descriptions, posts, job ads, policies, proposals. Most business writing is a variation on something you have already written ten times. Claude turns a few notes into a polished draft in seconds.

**Turning messy input into tidy output.** A page of meeting notes into action items. A pile of customer reviews into themes. A list of expenses into categories. A voice-memo transcript into a clear email. This kind of sorting and summarizing is where Claude is fastest and most reliable.

**First drafts of things you avoid.** The refund policy, the employee handbook, the marketing plan, the difficult email to a late-paying client. A good first draft removes the blank page, and that is often the whole battle.

**Thinking out loud.** Asking "what am I missing?" about a price rise, a new product or a hiring decision. Claude is a tireless sounding board that will list options, risks and questions you had not considered.

**Consistency.** Once Claude knows your business and your voice (Chapter 2), every customer reply, post and email sounds like the same business, even when different people write them.

## Where It Will Not Help (Much)

- **It does not know your numbers.** Claude cannot see your bank account, your till or your stock unless you paste the figures in or connect a tool. It can organize and explain numbers; it cannot make them up correctly.
- **It is not a professional adviser.** It can explain what a tax term means and prepare your questions for an accountant. It should not be the final word on tax, law or employment.
- **It does not replace your judgment about people.** It can draft an interview scorecard. It should not decide who you hire.
- **It can be wrong.** It sometimes invents facts, prices, laws or statistics. Every workflow in this book is designed so you check the parts that matter.

## The Hours Add Up

Here is what a typical week might look like for a small business owner using ten of the workflows in this book. The figures are estimates for a fictional bakery owner, not promises:

| Job | By hand | With Claude |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weekly email newsletter | 90 min | 20 min |
| Five social media posts | 75 min | 15 min |
| Replying to 30 customer emails | 120 min | 50 min |
| Chasing three late invoices | 30 min | 8 min |
| Sorting the week's receipts | 45 min | 15 min |
| Notes from two meetings | 40 min | 10 min |
| **Total** | **6 h 40 min** | **just under 2 h** |

That is roughly half a working day back, every week. The trick is not any single clever prompt. It is building a handful of workflows into your routine, which is what the rest of the book helps you do.

## A Note on the Name

This book is about Claude, the AI assistant made by Anthropic, because it is particularly good at writing in a natural voice, following detailed instructions, and working with long documents. Most of the prompts will also work in other AI assistants with small changes. Features, plan names and prices change often; where this book describes the Claude app, it describes it as it was at the time of writing, and Chapter 2 tells you what to look for if your screen looks different.

## Key Points

- Claude saves the most time on writing, summarizing, organizing and first drafts.
- It does not know your numbers or replace a professional adviser.
- A handful of workflows used every week saves more time than one clever prompt.
- **Claude drafts, you decide.**
