# Chapter 2: Setting Up Claude for Your Business

Twenty minutes of setup will make every workflow in this book better. The goal is simple: Claude should know who you are, what you sell, who your customers are and how you like to sound, without you explaining it every time.

## Step 1: Choose a Plan

Claude has a **free plan** and several **paid plans**. At the time of writing, the free plan lets you try every workflow in this book, but with daily usage limits. The free plan also includes a small number of **Projects** (Step 3), enough for the setup in this chapter. The individual paid plan (around $20 a month at the time of writing) raises the usage limits, removes the cap on Projects and adds more features for business use. Team plans add shared workspaces and admin controls for businesses with several people.

A sensible path:

1. Start on the free plan and try five workflows from the chapter that matters most to you.
2. If you hit the daily limit often, or need more Projects than the free plan allows, upgrade to the individual paid plan.
3. Consider a team plan only when two or more people in the business use Claude every day.

> **Note:** Plan names, prices and limits change often. Check the current details at claude.ai (or in the app's settings) before you buy.

## Step 2: Get the App

You can use Claude in three places, and your chats are shared between them:

- **On the web:** go to claude.ai and sign in.
- **On a computer:** download the desktop app for Mac or Windows from the same site.
- **On a phone:** install the Claude app from your phone's app store. Check that the maker is **Anthropic** before you install.

The desktop or web app is best for longer work like proposals and spreadsheets. The phone app is handy for quick replies, voice notes and photos of receipts.

## Step 3: Create a Business Project

A **Project** is a workspace inside Claude with its own instructions and files. Every chat you start inside the Project automatically knows what is in it. This is the single most useful setup step in this book.

1. In the left-hand menu, choose **Projects**, then **Create project** (the exact words may differ slightly).
2. Name it after your business, for example *Northbrook Bakery*.
3. In the **instructions** box (sometimes called *Project instructions* or *custom instructions*), paste your business profile from Step 4.
4. In the **files** or **knowledge** area, add documents Claude should know about: your price list, menu or service list, FAQ, return policy, and any past emails or posts you like.

From now on, start your business chats inside this Project. When a workflow in this book says "Claude already knows your business", this is what makes that true.

> **Tip:** Plans and their limits change. Check the current details on the Claude website before you pay. If you ever run out of Projects, keep your business profile in a note and paste it at the top of a new chat whenever you start a workflow. It works almost as well.

## Step 4: Write Your Business Profile

Claude can help you write it. Start a chat and use this prompt:

```
Please interview me, one question at a time, to write a one-page
business profile I can give an AI assistant. Cover: what my business
does, where it is, my products or services and prices, my typical
customers, what makes us different, how we like to sound (tone of
voice), words and phrases we always or never use, and anything the
assistant must never say or promise. When you have enough, write the
profile as a tidy list with headings.
```

Answer each question in a sentence or two. When Claude writes the profile, read it carefully, correct anything that is not quite right, and save it. The bonus files include a **Business Profile template** if you prefer to fill it in yourself.

Here is a shortened example for the fictional bakery we will meet throughout the book:

```result
BUSINESS PROFILE: NORTHBROOK BAKERY

What we do: Family bakery in Northbrook, open Tuesday to Sunday,
7 a.m. to 3 p.m. Sourdough, pastries, celebration cakes to order.

Customers: Local families, office workers on weekday mornings,
weekend visitors from nearby towns.

What makes us different: Everything is baked on site from 4 a.m.
Cakes are made to order with at least 5 days' notice.

How we sound: Warm, friendly, a little playful. Short sentences.
We say "folks" and "fresh out of the oven". We never use the words
"artisanal" or "world-class".

Never: promise delivery outside a 10-mile radius, give allergy
guarantees (we bake with nuts on site), or offer discounts over 15%.
```

## Step 5: Teach Claude Your Voice

If you have emails, posts or newsletters you are proud of, paste two or three into your Project's files or into a chat, and ask:

```
Here are three examples of my writing. Please describe my tone of
voice in five short bullet points, with words I use often and words
I never use. I will use your description as a style guide.
```

Add the result to your business profile, or use it to fill in the **Brand Voice Guide** template from the bonus files. Many users find this one step is the difference between "it sounds like a robot" and "it sounds like me."

## Step 6: A Few Settings Worth Checking

Look in **Settings** for:

- **Profile or personal preferences:** a box where you can tell Claude about yourself in every chat (for example, "I run a bakery; keep answers short and practical").
- **Privacy or data controls:** whether your chats may be used to improve the service. Choose the setting your business is comfortable with. Chapter 4 says more about privacy.
- **Connectors or integrations:** optional links to your email, calendar and documents. Chapter 16 covers these; you can ignore them for now.

> **Check First:** The names and places of these settings change as the app is updated. If you cannot find one, ask Claude itself: "Where do I find the privacy settings in this app?"

## Key Points

- Start free (the free plan includes a few Projects); upgrade when you keep hitting the limits.
- Create a Project for your business with a profile and your key documents.
- Teach Claude your voice from your best existing writing.
- Check the privacy settings before you add customer information.
