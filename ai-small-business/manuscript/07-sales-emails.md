# Chapter 7: Sales Emails and Follow-Ups

Most small businesses lose sales not because customers say no, but because nobody followed up. These workflows write the emails you mean to send: first contact, follow-ups, quotes, win-backs and the awkward money conversations.

## Workflow 21: Write a Cold Email to a Potential Client

**Use it when:** you want to approach a business or person you have not worked with before.

> **Time Saved:** About 35 minutes (from 45 down to 10).

```
Write a short first email from me to [name and role] at [their
business]. What I know about them: [details, for example they just
opened a second shop]. What I offer: [service]. Why it might help
them: [reason]. Ask for one small next step: [a 15-minute call,
a sample, a visit]. Under 120 words, friendly, no hype, no
"I hope this email finds you well". Give three subject lines.
```

```result
Subject: Fresh bread for your new café?

Hi Priya,

Congratulations on opening the second Copper Pot! I run Northbrook
Bakery, two streets away. We bake sourdough and pastries for three
local cafés, delivered by 7 a.m.

Would it help to try a free box this Friday? If you like it, I can
send prices. No pressure either way.

Best,
Sam
```

**Make it better:** ask Claude for a version that is 30% shorter. Shorter first emails usually get more replies.

> **Check First:** Rules on unsolicited business email differ by country. Send to business contacts with a genuine reason to hear from you, and offer an easy way to say no.

**Variations:**

- **LinkedIn message:** ask for a version under 300 characters.
- **Partnership idea:** ask for an email proposing a joint offer with a neighboring business.

## Workflow 22: Follow Up When There's No Reply

**Use it when:** you sent a quote or proposal a week ago and heard nothing.

> **Time Saved:** About 20 minutes per sequence (from 25 down to 5).

```
I sent [what] to [who] on [date] and haven't heard back. Write three
follow-up emails: one for 3 days later, one for 10 days later, and
a final polite "closing the loop" email for 3 weeks later. Each
under 70 words, friendly, adding something useful each time (a
tip, a deadline, a different option). Never guilt-trip.
```

```result
Day 3: "Hi Tom, just checking the quote reached you. Happy to adjust
the timing if early spring suits better."
Day 10: "Quick note - our March calendar is filling up. If you'd
like the 18th, I can hold it until Friday."
Week 3: "I'll assume the timing isn't right for now and close this
off. If anything changes, just reply - the quote's good until
April 30."
```

**Make it better:** save this sequence in your Project, and use it for every quote you send.

**Variations:**

- **After a meeting:** ask for a same-day follow-up that summarizes what you agreed.
- **After an event:** ask for a "great to meet you" email that mentions what you talked about.

## Workflow 23: Write a Quote Cover Email

**Use it when:** you are sending a price and want the email to sell the value, not just state a number.

> **Time Saved:** About 20 minutes (from 30 down to 10).

```
Write the email to go with my quote for [job] for [client]. Price:
[amount], valid until [date]. What's included: [list]. What's not
included: [list]. Why us: [two reasons]. Next step: [how to accept].
Under 180 words. Make the price easy to understand and the next
step obvious.
```

```result
Subject: Your quote for the bathroom refit - $6,800

Hi Lena,

Thanks for showing me round on Tuesday. Your quote is attached.

In short: $6,800 for the full refit, including the new shower,
tiling, and removal of the old suite. Paint and the mirror aren't
included.

We can start on March 18 and finish in eight working days...
To go ahead, just reply "yes" and I'll send the booking form.
```

**Make it better:** ask Claude to list three questions the client might have about the quote, and add short answers to the email.

> **Check First:** Check every price, date and inclusion against your actual quote.

**Variations:**

- **Three options:** ask for an email presenting good, better and best packages.
- **Repeat client:** ask for a shorter, warmer version that thanks them for coming back.

## Workflow 24: Win Back Past Customers

**Use it when:** you have customers who used to buy and have gone quiet.

> **Time Saved:** About 45 minutes (from 1 hour down to 15).

```
Write a short "we miss you" email for customers who haven't bought
from [business] in [time]. Mention what's new since they last
visited: [news]. Include one small, time-limited reason to come
back: [offer]. Warm, not desperate, under 120 words. Give three
subject lines, and a version for text message under 160 characters.
```

```result
Subject: It's been a while (and we've been busy)

Hi [first name],

We haven't seen you since the summer, and a lot has changed: a new
seeded rye, Saturday baking classes, and the same cinnamon buns.

Come by before November 30 and your coffee's on us.

Text version: "Northbrook Bakery here! Free coffee with any pastry
before Nov 30 - just show this text. Reply STOP to opt out."
```

> **Check First:** Only email or text customers who have agreed to hear from you, and include an easy way to opt out.

**Make it better:** ask for a second email for customers who don't respond, with a different angle.

**Variations:**

- **Subscription business:** ask for a "pause instead of cancel" email.
- **Service business:** ask for a "time for your annual check?" reminder.

## Workflow 25: Ask for a Referral

**Use it when:** a happy customer would probably recommend you, if you asked nicely.

> **Time Saved:** About 20 minutes (from 25 down to 5).

```
Write a short, natural email asking [customer] for a referral. We
just [finished job / delivered order] and they said [what they
said]. Ask if they know anyone else who might need [service]. Offer
[referral thank-you, if any]. Make it easy to say no. Under 100
words.
```

```result
Hi James,

So glad the garden came out the way you pictured it. Thanks for the
kind words on Friday!

Most of our work comes through people like you. If you know anyone
nearby who's been meaning to sort their garden, I'd be grateful for
an introduction. As a thank-you, your next spring tidy-up is half
price.

No worries at all if not - enjoy the new borders!
```

**Make it better:** ask Claude to also write a short message the customer can forward, so referring you takes them ten seconds.

**Variations:**

- **Ask for a review instead:** see Workflow 38.
- **B2B:** ask for an introduction to a specific type of company, not "anyone".

## Workflow 26: Chase a Late Payment

**Use it when:** an invoice is overdue and you dread writing the email.

> **Time Saved:** About 25 minutes per invoice (from 30 down to 5).

```
Write three emails to chase an overdue invoice. Client: [name].
Invoice: [number], amount [amount], due [date]. Email 1: friendly
reminder (assume they forgot). Email 2: one week later, firmer,
asking for a payment date. Email 3: two weeks later, final notice
stating [your next step, for example late fees as in our terms, or
pausing work]. Polite and professional throughout. No threats I
can't carry out.
```

```result
Email 1: "Hi Mark, a quick reminder that invoice 1042 for $1,250 was
due on March 3. I've attached it again in case it went astray.
Thanks!"
Email 2: "Hi Mark, invoice 1042 ($1,250) is now 10 days overdue.
Could you let me know when we can expect payment?"
Email 3: "Hi Mark, invoice 1042 remains unpaid... As set out in our
terms, a late fee of..."
```

> **Check First:** Only mention late fees or interest if your contract or terms allow them. For larger debts, ask a professional about your options before threatening action.

**Make it better:** add a line with a payment link or bank details placeholder, so paying is one click.

**Variations:**

- **Long-standing client:** ask for a warmer version that asks if anything is wrong.
- **Payment plan:** ask for an email offering to split the balance into installments.

## Workflow 27: Announce a Price Increase

**Use it when:** your costs have gone up and you need to raise prices without losing good customers.

> **Time Saved:** About 1 hour (from 75 minutes down to 15).

```
Write an email telling customers that our prices will rise by
[amount or percentage] from [date]. Reason: [honest reason]. What
stays the same: [quality, service, etc.]. Anything we're doing to
soften it: [for example, current prices until date, loyalty
discount]. Honest, brief, no long apology. Under 150 words. Then
write a short version for a sign or a social post.
```

```result
Subject: A small change to our prices from April 1

Hi [first name],

From April 1, our loaves will go up by 50 cents. Flour and energy
costs have risen sharply this year, and we'd rather adjust prices
than change our recipes.

Nothing else changes: same bakers, same 4 a.m. start, same bread.
Loyalty card holders keep today's prices until May 1.

Thank you for supporting a small bakery.
```

**Make it better:** ask Claude to list the three objections customers might raise, with short, calm answers for your staff.

**Variations:**

- **Contracted clients:** ask for a formal letter that references the notice period in your agreement.
- **Subscription:** ask for an email offering to lock in the old price for an annual plan.

## Workflow 28: Thank a New Customer

**Use it when:** someone has just bought for the first time and you want them to come back.

> **Time Saved:** About 20 minutes to write once, then seconds each time.

```
Write a short welcome email for new customers of [business] after
their first [purchase / booking]. Thank them, tell them one useful
thing to get the most from what they bought: [tip], invite them to
[next step: follow us, join the list, book again], and tell them how
to reach us. Under 120 words, warm, no hard sell.
```

```result
Subject: Thanks for your first order!

Hi [first name],

Thanks for ordering from Northbrook Bakery. A quick tip: keep your
sourdough in a tea towel, not plastic. It stays crusty for three
days.

Your loyalty card is on the app - every sixth loaf is on us.
Questions? Just reply to this email.

Sam and the team
```

**Make it better:** ask for a second email to send a week later, asking how it went.

**Variations:**

- **Service business:** ask for a "what happens next" email that explains the steps after booking.
- **High-value sale:** ask for a handwritten-card version under 40 words.

## Workflow 29: Write a Sales Page for One Product or Service

**Use it when:** you want a single page or long email that sells one thing well.

> **Time Saved:** About 2 hours (from 2.5 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Write a sales page for [product or service]. Who it's for: [customer].
Their problem: [problem]. What they get: [features, each with its
benefit]. Price: [price]. Proof: [real reviews or results, if any].
Common doubts: [doubts]. Structure: headline, the problem, the
solution, what's included, proof, answers to doubts, price and
guarantee [if any], call to action. Plain English, no hype, no
invented claims. Mark places where I should add a real photo or
quote.
```

```result
HEADLINE: Your birthday cake, exactly as you pictured it.
THE PROBLEM: Supermarket cakes look the same, and "custom" usually
means a name in icing...
WHAT'S INCLUDED: A design call, two sketches, tasting samples...
[ADD: photo of a past cake, with the customer's permission]
DOUBT: "What if it doesn't look like the picture?" ...
```

**Make it better:** ask Claude to read the page as a doubtful customer and list what would stop them from buying.

**Variations:**

- **Workshop or class:** ask for a page with the schedule, who it suits and what to bring.
- **Short version:** ask for a 150-word version for an email or a printed flyer.

## Workflow 30: Prepare for a Sales Call

**Use it when:** you have an important call or meeting with a potential client.

> **Time Saved:** About 40 minutes (from 50 down to 10).

```
I have a sales call with [who] about [what]. What I know: [notes].
Help me prepare: 1) five good questions to understand their needs;
2) the three objections they are most likely to raise, with calm
answers; 3) how to explain my price in one sentence; 4) a clear
next step to propose at the end. Keep it on one page.
```

```result
Questions:
1. "What made you start looking for a new supplier now?"
2. "What does a great Monday morning delivery look like for you?"
...
Likely objection: "Your bread costs more than our current supplier."
Answer: "It does. It also lasts two days longer, so most cafés
find they waste less. Shall we compare over a two-week trial?"
Next step: propose a two-week trial starting Monday.
```

**Make it better:** after the call, paste your notes and use Workflow 22 or Workflow 84 to send the follow-up within the hour.

**Variations:**

- **Renewal call:** ask for questions that find out what they want more of next year.
- **Negotiation:** ask Claude to role-play the client, so you can practice your answers.
