# Chapter 18: Quick-Start Guides by Type of Business

Every business is different, but businesses of the same type tend to have the same headaches. This chapter picks the workflows that usually save the most time for ten common types of small business, with a short note on how to adapt them. Find your type (or the closest one), start with its first five, and add the rest as you go.

> **Tip:** If your business mixes types, such as a café that also does catering, read both sections and choose the five workflows that match your most time-consuming jobs.

## Shops and Retail

Shops juggle stock, staff, social media and a steady stream of customer questions.

- **Start with:** 12 (captions from a photo), 5 (product descriptions), 31 (answer a customer email), 44 (staff schedule), 42 (opening and closing checklists).
- **Then add:** 6 (promotions), 33 (review replies), 47 (organize a messy stock list), 58 (compare supplier quotes), 20 (posts for a slow day).
- **Adapt it:** give Claude your product list with prices in a Project file, so product descriptions and replies are always accurate.

## Cafés, Restaurants and Food Businesses

Food businesses work long hours with thin margins, so time and costs both matter.

- **Start with:** 11 (a month of posts), 33 (review replies), 44 (staff schedule), 42 (checklists), 57 (price a product).
- **Then add:** 45 (customer instructions, such as reheating or storage), 48 (plan an event), 70 (team training on allergens or service), 78 (summarize new regulations), 54 (cash flow forecast).
- **Adapt it:** never let Claude answer allergy or dietary questions from memory. Keep a checked allergen list in your Project, and put "say [CHECK] for any allergy question" in your Reply Rules.

## Trades and Home Services

Plumbers, electricians, builders, cleaners and gardeners spend their evenings on quotes, invoices and scheduling.

- **Start with:** 72 (detailed quote), 23 (quote cover email), 22 (follow up with no reply), 26 (chase a late payment), 43 (email a supplier).
- **Then add:** 73 (case study), 25 (ask for a referral), 45 (aftercare instructions), 50 (contingency plan), 52 (receipt photos into a table).
- **Adapt it:** use your phone's voice input on the drive home: describe the job out loud and ask Claude to turn it into a quote and cover email.

## Salons, Spas and Personal Care

Appointment businesses live on rebookings, reviews and a full diary.

- **Start with:** 12 (captions from a photo), 38 (ask for reviews), 24 (win back past customers), 37 (calm an angry message), 35 (cancellation policy).
- **Then add:** 15 (short video scripts), 28 (thank a new customer), 45 (aftercare instructions), 44 (staff schedule), 39 (feedback survey).
- **Adapt it:** write your cancellation and no-show policy once (Workflow 35's service-business variation) and add it to your Reply Rules, so every reply quotes it the same way.

## Consultants, Coaches and Freelancers

When you are the business, every hour spent on admin is an hour you can't bill.

- **Start with:** 21 (cold email), 71 (proposal), 84 (meeting follow-up), 30 (prepare for a sales call), 90 (monthly review).
- **Then add:** 73 (case study), 76 (about page and bio), 29 (sales page), 13 (repurpose one piece of content), 97 (pitch or presentation).
- **Adapt it:** keep a "client file" Project for each major client with only the information they've agreed you can use, and remove it when the work ends.

## Professional Offices

Accountants, lawyers, agents and other professional firms handle sensitive information, so the rules come first.

- **Start with:** 80 (proofread a document), 78 (summarize a document), 83 (meeting agenda), 40 (sort your inbox, using subject lines only), 87 (delegate a task).
- **Then add:** 4 (newsletter), 34 (FAQ page), 67 (staff handbook section), 76 (bios), 66 (onboarding plan).
- **Adapt it:** check your professional body's rules on AI and client confidentiality before you start, and keep client information out unless your firm's agreement and policy allow it (Chapter 4).

## Online Shops

Online sellers write endless product listings, answer the same order questions and fight for attention.

- **Start with:** 5 (product descriptions), 36 (saved replies), 31 (answer a customer email), 35 (returns policy), 16 (hashtags and keywords).
- **Then add:** 4 (newsletter), 24 (win back past customers), 7 (insights from reviews), 99 (online presence review), 18 (giveaway).
- **Adapt it:** if your shop platform exports orders or reviews as a spreadsheet, attach the file and ask Claude to find patterns, such as the most common reasons for returns.

## Fitness, Classes and Tutoring

Instructors, studios and tutors run on timetables, bookings and keeping members motivated.

- **Start with:** 11 (a month of posts), 15 (short video scripts), 28 (welcome new customers), 24 (win back lapsed members), 39 (feedback survey).
- **Then add:** 48 (plan an event or workshop), 29 (sales page for a course), 70 (training for instructors), 86 (quarterly goals), 96 (ways to grow).
- **Adapt it:** never ask Claude to give individual health, injury or medical advice to clients; use it for scheduling, marketing and general information, and refer health questions to qualified professionals.

## Creative Businesses

Photographers, designers, makers and studios need to market their work while protecting their creative time.

- **Start with:** 76 (about page and bio), 12 (captions from a photo), 71 (proposal), 74 (simple terms), 26 (chase a late payment).
- **Then add:** 73 (case study), 21 (cold email), 2 (one-sentence pitch), 18 (giveaway), 100 (annual plan).
- **Adapt it:** use Claude for the words around your work, such as descriptions, proposals and terms, and keep the creative work itself yours.

## Property and Hospitality

Vacation rentals, guesthouses, small hotels and property managers answer guest questions around the clock.

- **Start with:** 36 (saved replies), 45 (guest instructions), 33 (review replies), 42 (changeover checklist), 31 (answer a guest message).
- **Then add:** 9 (website home page), 50 (contingency plan), 43 (email a supplier or contractor), 58 (compare quotes), 99 (online presence review).
- **Adapt it:** create a "house book" file with check-in details, Wi-Fi, rules and local tips, and let Claude draft guest replies only from that file.

## Any Business: The Universal Five

If none of these fits, or you're not sure where to start, these five workflows help almost every business in the first month:

1. **Workflow 31:** Answer a customer email.
2. **Workflow 26:** Chase a late payment.
3. **Workflow 81:** Plan your day.
4. **Workflow 84:** Turn meeting notes into a follow-up email.
5. **Workflow 90:** Do a monthly business review.
