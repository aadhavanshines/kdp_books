# Chapter 11: Hiring and Your Team

Hiring well and keeping good people are among the most important things a small business does, and among the most time-consuming. Claude can draft job ads, interview questions, onboarding plans and difficult messages. It must never be the one making decisions about people.

> **Check First:** Employment law varies by country and state. Use Claude for drafts and preparation; have contracts, policies, warnings and dismissals checked by an HR professional or employment lawyer. Don't paste candidates' or employees' personal details, and never ask Claude to screen, rank or reject candidates.

## Workflow 61: Write a Job Description

**Use it when:** you need to hire and don't have a clear description of the role.

> **Time Saved:** About 1 hour (from 75 minutes down to 15).

```
Write a job description for a [role] at [business]. Hours: [hours].
Pay: [pay]. Main tasks: [list]. Must-have skills: [list]. Nice-to-
have: [list]. What it's like to work here: [honest description].
Use plain, inclusive language, avoid jargon and words that put
people off applying, and list only requirements that are truly
needed.
```

```result
EARLY BAKER (Part-time, 4 a.m. - 10 a.m., Thursday to Sunday)
$19 an hour, plus free bread (seriously, a lot of it)

You'll help shape, prove and bake our bread and pastries before the
shop opens...
You'll need: to be comfortable with early starts and lifting 25 kg
flour sacks; reliable transport at 4 a.m.
Nice to have: some kitchen or bakery experience. We'll teach you
the rest.
```

**Make it better:** ask Claude to check the description for any wording that might discourage particular groups from applying.

**Variations:**

- **Freelancer or contractor:** ask for a project brief instead of a job description.
- **Internship or apprenticeship:** ask for a version that stresses training and learning.

## Workflow 62: Write a Job Ad

**Use it when:** you have a job description and need a short ad for a job site, social media or a sign in the window.

> **Time Saved:** About 30 minutes (from 40 down to 10).

```
Turn this job description into three job ads: 1) a job-site ad under
200 words; 2) a social media post under 80 words; 3) a window or
bulletin board sign under 40 words. Lead with what makes the job
attractive, include pay and hours, and say exactly how to apply.

[paste the job description]
```

```result
WINDOW SIGN:
"We're hiring! Early baker, Thu-Sun, 4-10 a.m. $19/hour + free
bread. No experience needed - we'll train you. Ask inside or email
jobs@northbrook.example"
```

**Make it better:** ask for three headline options and test which gets more applications.

**Variations:**

- **Hard-to-fill role:** ask Claude to suggest where people like this look for work.
- **Seasonal staff:** ask for an ad that stresses flexibility and the end date.

## Workflow 63: Prepare Interview Questions

**Use it when:** you have interviews coming up and want fair, useful questions.

> **Time Saved:** About 45 minutes (from 1 hour down to 15).

```
Write interview questions for a [role]. The skills and qualities
that matter most: [list]. For each, give two questions that ask for
real examples ("Tell me about a time..."), and what a good answer
would include. Add three questions the candidate is likely to ask me,
with suggested honest answers. Avoid any questions about age,
family, health, religion or other personal matters.
```

```result
RELIABILITY
Q: "Tell me about a time you had to be somewhere very early, day
after day. How did you make it work?"
Good answer: specific routine, backup plan, honest about challenges.
CANDIDATE MIGHT ASK: "Is there a chance of more hours later?"
Honest answer: "Possibly in the spring; I can't promise it now."
```

**Make it better:** ask every candidate the same core questions, so you compare like with like.

**Variations:**

- **Phone screen:** ask for five short questions for a 10-minute first call.
- **Practical test:** ask for a short, fair task that shows the real skill (for example, shaping five rolls).

## Workflow 64: Create an Interview Scorecard

**Use it when:** you want to compare candidates fairly instead of going with your gut.

> **Time Saved:** About 30 minutes (from 40 down to 10).

```
Create an interview scorecard for a [role]. Use these criteria:
[list skills and qualities]. For each, define what a score of 1, 3
and 5 looks like in one line each. Add space for evidence notes.
Keep it on one page. Then write three tips for scoring fairly.
```

```result
CRITERION: Working under pressure
1 = No example given, or became flustered in the example.
3 = Gave an example; coped, but didn't describe how.
5 = Clear example with specific actions and a good outcome.
Evidence notes: ______________________
Tips: score straight after each interview; use evidence, not
impressions; score each criterion before giving an overall view.
```

**Make it better:** use the bonus **Hiring Scorecard** template and fill it in yourself during or right after each interview.

> **Check First:** You score the candidates. Don't paste candidates' answers into Claude and ask it to rank them.

**Variations:**

- **Panel interviews:** ask for a version where each interviewer scores independently.
- **Internal promotion:** ask for criteria for the new role, not the old one.

## Workflow 65: Write an Offer Letter and Welcome Email

**Use it when:** you have chosen someone and want to welcome them properly.

> **Time Saved:** About 40 minutes (from 50 down to 10), before your professional check of the formal terms.

```
Draft an offer letter for [role], starting [date], [hours], [pay],
reporting to [name], with [probation period / benefits]. Warm but
clear. Then a separate welcome email for their first day: where to
go, what time, what to wear, what to bring, who they'll meet, and
what the first week looks like. Mark any terms that should be
checked against employment law and their contract.
```

```result
WELCOME EMAIL
Subject: See you Thursday at 4 a.m. (yes, really!)

Hi Tom,

We're delighted you're joining us. On Thursday, come to the back
door on Mill Lane at 3:55 a.m. Wear comfortable shoes with closed
toes; we'll give you an apron and a hat...
```

**Make it better:** ask Claude for a list of everything to prepare before day one (keys, uniform, accounts, paperwork).

**Variations:**

- **Unsuccessful candidates:** ask for a kind, short "thanks but no" email that thanks them for their time.
- **Contractor:** ask for a welcome pack instead of an offer letter.

## Workflow 66: Plan a New Hire's First Two Weeks

**Use it when:** new people are thrown in at the deep end and you'd like them to settle faster.

> **Time Saved:** About 1.5 hours (from 2 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Create a two-week onboarding plan for a new [role]. What they need
to learn: [tasks, systems, rules]. Who can train them: [names].
Give a day-by-day plan with one or two goals per day, who helps,
and a short check at the end of each week. Include safety training
and introductions on day one.
```

```result
DAY 1
Goals: safety walk-round; meet the team; watch the morning bake.
Who: Sam.
DAY 2
Goals: shape rolls with Leah; learn the proofer settings.
...
END OF WEEK 1 CHECK: Can Tom open the bakehouse safely with help?
Can he shape rolls to size?
```

**Make it better:** turn your procedures (Workflow 41) into the training materials for the plan.

**Variations:**

- **Remote worker:** ask for a plan with video calls, accounts and check-ins.
- **Seasonal staff:** ask for a one-day version that covers only the essentials.

## Workflow 67: Write a Staff Handbook Section

**Use it when:** your rules live in your head, and staff keep asking.

> **Time Saved:** About 1.5 hours per section (from 2 hours down to 30 minutes), before your professional check.

```
Draft a staff handbook section on [topic, for example vacation and
time off, sick days, uniform, phone use, breaks]. Our rules: [your
rules]. Write it in plain English, friendly and clear, with
headings, under 400 words. List anything I should check against
employment law where we operate.
```

```result
VACATION AND TIME OFF
How much: full-time staff get [number] days a year plus public
holidays...
How to ask: put your request in the vacation book at least four
weeks ahead...
TO CHECK: minimum paid vacation where you are; how vacation builds up
for part-time staff...
```

> **Check First:** Have the finished handbook checked by an HR professional before you rely on it.

**Make it better:** ask for a one-page summary of the most important rules for the break room wall.

**Variations:**

- **Social media policy:** ask what staff may and may not post about the business.
- **Health and safety:** ask for a section, then check it against official guidance for your industry.

## Workflow 68: Prepare for a Performance Conversation

**Use it when:** you need to talk with a team member about their work, good or bad.

> **Time Saved:** About 45 minutes (from 1 hour down to 15).

```
Help me prepare for a conversation with a team member about [topic].
What's happened: [facts, without names or personal details]. What I
want to happen: [outcome]. Give me: an opening line, the three key
points to make with specific examples, questions to understand their
side, how to agree next steps, and how to close. Calm, fair and
respectful.
```

```result
Opening: "Thanks for making time. I want to talk about mornings,
because I think something's not working, and I'd like to hear how
it looks from your side."
Points: three late starts this month (dates); the impact on the
8 a.m. café delivery...
Questions: "Is something making the early start harder at the
moment?"
Next steps: agree a plan and a check-in in two weeks.
```

> **Check First:** For formal warnings or anything that might lead to dismissal, follow a fair process and get professional advice first.

**Make it better:** ask Claude to play the team member, so you can practice the conversation.

**Variations:**

- **Praise:** ask for help giving specific, meaningful praise for great work.
- **Review meeting:** ask for an agenda and questions for a six-monthly review.

## Workflow 69: Write a Difficult Message to the Team

**Use it when:** you have news the team won't like: changes to hours, a closure, a new rule.

> **Time Saved:** About 40 minutes (from 50 down to 10).

```
I need to tell my team about [news]. Why it's happening: [honest
reasons]. What it means for them: [impact]. What we're doing to
help: [support]. Write a short message for [meeting / email / group
chat]: honest, kind, clear about what changes and when, and inviting
questions. Under 200 words. Then list five questions they may ask,
with honest answers.
```

```result
"Hi all, I want to tell you about a change before you hear it
anywhere else. From March, we'll close on Mondays. Monday sales have
fallen for a year and the shop now loses money that day. Nobody is
losing their job, and your total hours won't drop; we'll move them
to Saturday mornings, which are busier..."
```

**Make it better:** share big news in person first, then send the written version so everyone has the details.

**Variations:**

- **Good news:** ask for a message celebrating a milestone and thanking the team.
- **New system:** ask for a message introducing a new tool or process, with what changes for each person.

## Workflow 70: Plan Team Training

**Use it when:** you want your team to learn something new: a product, a skill, customer service, a new tool.

> **Time Saved:** About 1 hour (from 75 minutes down to 15).

```
Plan a [length] training session for my team on [topic]. Who: [roles,
experience]. What they should be able to do afterwards: [goals].
Give me an outline with timings, a short explanation for each part,
one practical activity, a five-question quiz, and a one-page
handout.
```

```result
SERVING CUSTOMERS WITH ALLERGIES (30 minutes)
0-5 min: Why it matters (a real near-miss story)...
5-15 min: Our process: always check the allergen folder, never guess
15-25 min: Role-play: three customer questions
25-30 min: Quiz and questions
QUIZ: 1. A customer asks if a muffin is nut-free. What do you do?
```

**Make it better:** after the session, ask Claude to turn the quiz into a short refresher you can repeat every three months.

**Variations:**

- **Training on Claude itself:** use Chapters 3 and 4 of this book as the source material.
- **Short video lessons:** ask for scripts for three two-minute training videos.
