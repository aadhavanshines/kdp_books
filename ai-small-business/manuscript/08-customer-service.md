# Chapter 8: Customer Service

Good customer service is mostly answering the same questions well, every time, and handling the occasional unhappy customer calmly. Claude is excellent at both, as long as it knows your real answers. Before you start, fill in the **Customer FAQ Sheet** and **Reply Rules** templates from the bonus files and add them to your Project.

## Workflow 31: Answer a Customer Email

**Use it when:** a customer has asked a question by email or message.

> **Time Saved:** About 5 minutes per email (from 7 down to 2), which adds up fast.

```
Draft a reply to this customer message. Use only the facts in my FAQ
sheet and reply rules. If the answer isn't there, say so and leave
a [CHECK] note for me instead of guessing. Warm, clear, under 120
words, signed [name].

[paste the customer's message]
```

```result
Hi Ana,

Thanks for getting in touch! Yes, we can make the chocolate cake for
12 people - it's $64 and needs 5 days' notice, so for Saturday the
14th we'd need your order by Monday.

[CHECK: she asked about a nut-free version - our FAQ says we can't
guarantee nut-free. Confirm wording.]

Sam
```

**Make it better:** paste five emails at once and ask for five replies. Batch replying is far faster than one at a time.

> **Check First:** Never let Claude give allergy, safety or medical guarantees. Those answers come from you.

**Variations:**

- **Phone script:** ask for a short spoken version your staff can use on the phone.
- **Another language:** ask Claude to reply in the customer's language, and keep an English copy for your records.

## Workflow 32: Handle a Complaint

**Use it when:** a customer is unhappy and you want to put it right without making it worse.

> **Time Saved:** About 25 minutes (from 35 down to 10), and a calmer reply than you might write while annoyed.

```
A customer has complained. Here's their message and what actually
happened: [your side, honestly]. Draft a reply that: thanks them,
apologizes once for what we got wrong (and only that), explains
briefly without blaming, offers [what we can offer under our reply
rules], and says what we'll do to stop it happening again. Calm,
human, under 150 words. Don't admit legal fault.

[paste the complaint]
```

```result
Hi Daniel,

Thank you for telling us, and I'm sorry your cake arrived with the
top layer damaged. That shouldn't happen, especially on a birthday.

Our driver took a corner too fast; we're adding a non-slip mat to
every delivery from today.

I'd like to make it right: we'll bake a replacement cake of your
choice, free, for any date in the next month. Just reply with the
date.

Sam, owner
```

**Make it better:** before sending, ask Claude "How might this customer read this reply? Is anything likely to annoy them?"

**Variations:**

- **Complaint you disagree with:** ask for a reply that is polite and firm, acknowledges their feelings and explains your policy.
- **Serious complaint (injury, safety, legal threat):** don't use AI to reply. Speak to your insurer or a legal adviser first.

## Workflow 33: Reply to Online Reviews

**Use it when:** you have new public reviews, good or bad, to answer.

> **Time Saved:** About 30 minutes a week (from 40 down to 10).

```
Write public replies to these reviews of [business]. Positive
reviews: thank them by first name, mention something specific they
said, under 50 words. Negative reviews: thank them, apologize
briefly for their experience, don't argue in public, give a way to
contact us directly, under 70 words. Never reveal private details
about the customer.

[paste reviews]
```

```result
5 stars, "Best croissant in the county": "Thank you, Lucy! Our
croissants take three days from start to finish, so this made the
whole team smile. See you soon."
2 stars, "Long line, rude staff": "Thanks for the honest feedback,
Raj, and I'm sorry your visit felt rushed. That's not how we want
anyone to feel. Could you email sam@northbrook.example? I'd like to
hear more and put it right."
```

**Make it better:** ask Claude for three different openings for positive replies, so your responses don't all start the same way.

**Variations:**

- **Fake or abusive review:** ask for a short, neutral reply and check the platform's process for reporting it.
- **Review with a factual error:** ask for a polite correction that doesn't sound defensive.

## Workflow 34: Build an FAQ Page

**Use it when:** customers keep asking the same questions and your website doesn't answer them.

> **Time Saved:** About 2 hours (from 2.5 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Below are customer questions from the last few months. Group them
into topics, remove duplicates, and write a website FAQ page with
clear, short answers using only the facts I give. Order the topics
by how often they come up. Where I haven't given an answer, write
[ANSWER NEEDED] instead of guessing.

Questions: [paste emails, messages, notes]
Facts: [hours, prices, policies, delivery areas, etc.]
```

```result
ORDERING CAKES
How much notice do you need? At least 5 days for celebration cakes,
and 2 weeks in December.
Can I change my order? Yes, up to 48 hours before collection.

ALLERGIES
Are your products nut-free? [ANSWER NEEDED]
```

**Make it better:** use the finished page as the FAQ sheet in your Project, so Claude's replies always match your website.

**Variations:**

- **In-store sign:** ask for the top five questions as a short printable sign.
- **New staff:** ask for a quiz version to test new team members.

## Workflow 35: Write a Returns or Refund Policy

**Use it when:** your policy is missing, unclear or causing arguments.

> **Time Saved:** About 1.5 hours (from 2 hours down to 30 minutes), before your professional check.

```
Help me write a clear returns and refund policy for [business] in
[country / state]. We sell [what], [online / in store / both]. What
I want: [your rules, for example 14 days for unused items, no
refunds on custom orders]. Write it in plain English with headings,
under 300 words. Then list anything I should check against
consumer law where I trade.
```

```result
RETURNS
You can return unopened, undamaged items within 14 days of delivery
for a full refund...
CUSTOM ORDERS
Celebration cakes made to your design can't be refunded once baking
has started, unless they are faulty...
TO CHECK: your local cooling-off rights for online orders; whether
"no refund" wording is allowed for faulty goods where you trade...
```

> **Check First:** Consumer law sets minimum rights that a policy cannot take away. Have the final policy checked against the rules where you trade.

**Make it better:** ask for a three-sentence summary to show at checkout or on receipts.

**Variations:**

- **Service business:** ask for a cancellation and rescheduling policy instead.
- **Subscription:** ask for a cancellation policy with clear notice periods.

## Workflow 36: Build a Library of Saved Replies

**Use it when:** you and your team type the same answers over and over.

> **Time Saved:** About 2 hours to build; then about 30 minutes a week.

```
Using my FAQ sheet and reply rules, write a library of 15 saved
replies for our most common customer messages: [list the types,
for example order status, delivery areas, booking changes, gift
cards]. Each: a short title, a reply under 80 words with
[placeholders] for details, and a note on when NOT to use it.
```

```result
1. WHERE'S MY ORDER?
Hi [name], thanks for checking in! Your order [number] is [status]
and should arrive by [date]. Here's the tracking link: [link].
Don't use when: the order is more than 3 days late - use the
apology reply instead.
```

**Make it better:** paste the library into your email program's templates or canned responses feature, so anyone can use it.

**Variations:**

- **Social media DMs:** ask for shorter versions under 40 words.
- **Phone:** ask for the same library as short spoken scripts.

## Workflow 37: Calm Down an Angry Message

**Use it when:** you have received a rude or furious message and need to reply professionally.

> **Time Saved:** About 20 minutes (from 30 down to 10), plus a good night's sleep.

```
I received the angry message below. First, tell me in two or three
bullet points what the customer actually wants, ignoring the tone.
Then draft a calm, short reply that addresses those points, sets a
polite boundary if they were abusive, and suggests a next step.
Don't match their tone and don't apologize for things we didn't do.

[paste the message]
```

```result
What they want:
- Their deposit back ($50)
- To know why their booking was moved

Reply:
"Hi Chris, I understand the change was frustrating. We moved your
booking because the stylist was ill, and we should have called
rather than texted. Your $50 deposit will be refunded within 3
working days. I'm happy to talk by phone if that would help, but
I'd ask that we keep messages respectful."
```

**Make it better:** if you're upset, wait an hour before sending, even if the draft is ready.

**Variations:**

- **Threats or harassment:** keep records, don't engage, and seek advice. AI can help you write a short, factual reply only after you've had that advice.
- **Angry staff member:** use Workflow 69 instead.

## Workflow 38: Ask Happy Customers for Reviews

**Use it when:** you have happy customers but few online reviews.

> **Time Saved:** About 30 minutes to set up; then seconds each time.

```
Write a short message asking a happy customer to leave a review of
[business] on [platform]. Send it [when, for example, the day after
their visit]. Thank them, make it easy (include [review link]),
suggest they mention [what they liked] only if they want to, and
make it fine to ignore. Write an email version and a text version
under 160 characters.
```

```result
Email: "Hi Megan, thanks again for choosing us for Ella's birthday
cake! If you have a minute, a short Google review would really help
a small bakery like ours: [link]. No worries if not - we hope the
party was wonderful."
Text: "Thanks for choosing Northbrook Bakery! A quick review would
mean a lot: [link]. Reply STOP to opt out."
```

> **Check First:** Don't offer rewards for reviews or ask only happy customers on platforms that forbid it ("review gating"). Check the platform's current rules.

**Make it better:** put the review link on your receipts and in your email signature too.

**Variations:**

- **B2B:** ask for a request for a short written testimonial you can use with permission.
- **Video review:** ask for a friendly request with three questions they could answer on camera.

## Workflow 39: Write a Customer Feedback Survey

**Use it when:** you want to learn what customers think, without a 30-question form nobody finishes.

> **Time Saved:** About 1 hour (from 75 minutes down to 15).

```
Write a short customer feedback survey for [business]. What I want
to learn: [goals, for example why people choose us, what would make
them visit more often]. Maximum 6 questions, mostly multiple choice
or rating scales, with one open question. Neutral wording that
doesn't lead the answer. Then write a two-sentence invitation to
take it.
```

```result
1. How often do you visit us? (Weekly / Monthly / A few times a
   year / This was my first visit)
2. What do you buy most often? (Bread / Pastries / Cakes / Coffee)
3. How likely are you to recommend us to a friend? (0-10)
...
6. If you could change one thing about Northbrook Bakery, what
   would it be?
```

**Make it better:** when the answers come back, paste them in and use Workflow 7 to find the themes.

**Variations:**

- **After a service:** ask for a three-question survey sent the day after.
- **Product idea test:** ask for a survey that tests interest in a new product before you make it.

## Workflow 40: Sort Your Inbox

**Use it when:** you open your inbox and don't know where to start.

> **Time Saved:** About 20 minutes a day (from 30 down to 10).

```
Below are the subject lines and first lines of my unread emails.
Sort them into: 1) urgent - reply today; 2) customers waiting for
an answer; 3) money (invoices, payments, bills); 4) can wait this
week; 5) newsletters and probable spam. For groups 1 and 2, suggest
a one-line reply for each.

[paste subjects and first lines, with personal details removed]
```

```result
URGENT
- "Order for tomorrow 8 a.m.?" (Copper Pot Café) -> "Yes, confirmed
  for 7:45 a.m. delivery."
CUSTOMERS WAITING
- "Gluten-free options?" -> use saved reply 4
MONEY
- Invoice 2231 from Mill Flour Co. ($812, due Friday)
CAN WAIT
- Market stall application deadline (Nov 30)
```

**Make it better:** if your plan supports email connectors (Chapter 16), Claude can read your inbox directly; start with read-only access and draft replies, never auto-send.

**Variations:**

- **Shared inbox:** ask Claude to suggest which team member should handle each email.
- **End of week:** ask for a list of every email still waiting for a reply.
