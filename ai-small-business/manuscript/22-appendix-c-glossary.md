# Appendix C: Glossary

Plain-English meanings of the AI and business terms used in this book.

**AI assistant.** A computer program you talk to in ordinary language, which can answer questions, write drafts and help with tasks. Claude is one.

**AI use policy.** A short set of rules for how your business uses AI tools: what information may go in, who checks the results, and what never gets automated (Chapter 4).

**Attachment.** A file, such as a PDF, photo or spreadsheet, that you add to a chat so Claude can read it.

**Automation.** Setting up a job to run by itself when something happens, such as a new form entry. In this book, automations start in draft mode.

**Brackets.** The [words in square brackets] in each prompt, which you replace with your own details.

**Brand voice.** How your business sounds in writing: friendly or formal, playful or plain, with the words you use and avoid.

**Business profile.** A short document with the key facts about your business, saved in a Project so Claude always knows them.

**Cash flow.** The money coming into and going out of the business over time. A business can be profitable and still run short of cash.

**Chat.** A single conversation with Claude. Starting a new chat gives it a fresh start, without the earlier conversation.

**Connector.** A link that lets Claude read, and sometimes act on, information in another service, such as your email, calendar or documents (Chapter 16).

**Draft mode.** A way of working where Claude prepares a message or document and a person checks it and decides whether to send it.

**FAQ.** Frequently asked questions: the questions customers ask most, with your answers.

**Gross margin.** The share of each sale left after paying the direct costs of making or buying what you sold.

**Hallucination.** When an AI assistant states something that sounds confident but is wrong or made up. The reason to check every fact.

**Integration.** Another word for a connection between two apps or services.

**Model.** The AI system behind an assistant. Different models vary in speed, cost and ability.

**No-code.** Tools that let you build things, such as automations, by clicking and filling in forms instead of writing computer code.

**Onboarding.** Helping a new team member, or a new customer, get started.

**Plan (subscription).** The level of service you pay for. Higher plans usually allow more use and more features.

**Profit and loss report.** A summary of sales, costs and profit over a period. Also called an income statement.

**Project.** A workspace in Claude with its own instructions and files, so you don't have to repeat background information in every chat.

**Prompt.** The message you type to an AI assistant, explaining what you want.

**Read-only access.** Permission to look at information without changing, sending or deleting anything. The safest way to start with connectors.

**Rota.** A staff schedule showing who works when. Also called a roster or schedule.

**Saved reply.** A ready-written answer to a common question, with spaces for the details. Also called a canned response or template.

**SOP (standard operating procedure).** Step-by-step written instructions for how a job is done in your business.

**SWOT analysis.** A simple review of a business's strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats.

**Template.** A ready-made document with blanks to fill in.

**Time Saved note.** The estimate at the start of each workflow of how much time it usually saves. Your results may differ.

**Workflow.** In this book: a repeatable job, with a prompt, an example result and tips, that Claude helps you do faster.
