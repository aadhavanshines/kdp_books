# Chapter 6: Social Media

Social media rewards consistency more than brilliance. These workflows help you post regularly without staring at a blank caption box, and reply to comments without it eating your evening.

## Workflow 11: Plan a Month of Posts

**Use it when:** you post when you remember, and want a simple plan you can actually stick to.

> **Time Saved:** About 1.5 hours (from 2 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Plan a month of social media posts for [business] on [platforms].
I can post [number] times a week. Mix: 40% behind the scenes, 30%
products or services, 20% customers and community, 10% offers.
Include dates that matter for my business in [month]. Give me a
table: date, platform, post idea, what photo or video I need, and a
one-line hook. Keep every idea doable with a phone in 10 minutes.
```

```result
| Date | Platform | Idea | Photo/video | Hook |
| Tue 1 | Instagram | Shaping the morning loaves | 15-sec video | "4:12 a.m. Somebody has to do it." |
| Thu 3 | Facebook | Meet Maria, our cake decorator | Portrait at her bench | "The hands behind every birthday cake." |
| Sat 5 | Instagram | Customer's wedding cake | Their photo (with permission) | "Two years of planning. One cake." |
```

**Make it better:** ask Claude to turn the plan into a checklist you can check off, with the photo list grouped so you can shoot a week's photos in one go.

**Variations:**

- **No time for video:** say "photos and text only" and ask for ideas that work as a single still image.
- **LinkedIn-focused business:** ask for a mix of tips, lessons learned and client results instead of behind-the-scenes posts.

## Workflow 12: Write Captions From a Photo

**Use it when:** you have a good photo and no idea what to say.

> **Time Saved:** About 15 minutes per post (from 20 down to 5).

```
Here is a photo for a social post for [business] on [platform].
[attach the photo] It shows [describe it if needed]. Write three
caption options in our voice: one short and punchy, one that tells
a tiny story, and one that asks followers a question. Each under
[number] words, with a clear call to action and up to [number]
relevant hashtags.
```

```result
1. Short: "Cinnamon buns, 7 a.m., still warm. You know what to do."
2. Story: "Every Saturday Grace orders two cinnamon buns 'for her
   husband'. He's never been in. We've stopped asking."
3. Question: "Cinnamon or cardamom? Settle this for us below."
#Northbrook #FreshBaked #WeekendTreat
```

**Make it better:** keep your best-performing captions in your Project's files, and ask Claude to write new ones "in the style of these".

**Variations:**

- **Before and after photos (trades, salons, cleaning):** ask for a caption that explains the problem, the fix and the time it took.
- **Product photo:** ask for a caption that answers the question a customer would ask most about it.

## Workflow 13: Repurpose One Piece of Content Five Ways

**Use it when:** you have written something good once, like a newsletter, a blog post or a talk, and want more mileage from it.

> **Time Saved:** About 1.5 hours (from 2 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Below is a [newsletter / blog post / talk transcript]. Turn it into:
1) an Instagram caption, 2) a Facebook post, 3) a LinkedIn post,
4) three short tips for a carousel (one sentence each, plus a cover
title), 5) a 30-second script for a talking-to-camera video. Keep
our voice and the key message: [main point]. Don't add facts that
aren't in the original.

[paste the content]
```

```result
4) CAROUSEL
Cover: "3 things we wish customers knew about sourdough"
Slide 1: "It's not sour by accident. The tang comes from 18 hours
of slow rising."
Slide 2: "It keeps better than you think: wrap it in a dish towel,
not plastic."
Slide 3: "Stale? Slice, toast, and it's back to its best."
```

**Make it better:** ask Claude to space the five pieces over two weeks, with the strongest one first.

**Variations:**

- **From a customer FAQ:** turn your five most common questions into a week of posts.
- **From a video:** paste the transcript and ask for a caption plus three quote cards.

## Workflow 14: Reply to Comments and Messages

**Use it when:** comments and direct messages pile up and you want friendly, consistent replies.

> **Time Saved:** About 30 minutes a day (from 45 down to 15).

```
Here are [number] comments and messages from our social media.
Draft a short reply to each in our voice: friendly, human, under
40 words. Answer questions using only these facts: [hours, prices,
links, policies]. If a comment is a complaint, reply politely and
invite them to message us privately. If a comment is abusive or
spam, write "SKIP" and say why.

[paste comments, with usernames replaced by numbers]
```

```result
1. "Do you do gluten-free?" -> "We do two gluten-free cakes, but we
   bake with flour on site so we can't promise they're allergen-free.
   Send us a message and we'll talk you through it!"
2. "Waited 20 mins on Saturday" -> "Sorry about the wait! Saturdays
   are busy - send us a message, we'd love to make it up to you."
3. "Click here for free followers" -> SKIP (spam).
```

**Make it better:** add your FAQ sheet (bonus template 3) to your Project so every reply uses your real answers.

> **Check First:** Read each reply before posting, especially anything about allergies, prices or refunds.

**Variations:**

- **Reviews on review sites:** see Workflow 33 for public review replies.
- **Busy season:** ask for a pinned post that answers the five questions you get most often.

## Workflow 15: Write Short Video Scripts

**Use it when:** you know video gets more reach but freeze when the camera starts.

> **Time Saved:** About 40 minutes per video (from 50 down to 10).

```
Write three scripts for 30-second vertical videos for [business].
Topic ideas: [topics or "suggest some"]. Each script: a hook in the
first 3 seconds, three short beats, and a call to action. Show what
to film for each line in brackets. Natural spoken English, as if
talking to a friend. No more than 75 words of speech each.
```

```result
SCRIPT 1: "Why our bread costs more"
[Close-up: dough being folded] HOOK: "This loaf took 18 hours."
[Wide: empty bakery at 4 a.m.] "We start before the sun..."
[Hands shaping] "Every loaf shaped by hand..."
[Slice, steam rising] "That's the difference. Taste it Saturday."
```

**Make it better:** ask for on-screen text for each beat, for people watching with the sound off.

**Variations:**

- **Shy on camera:** ask for "hands only" or "voice-over" scripts where you never appear.
- **Explaining a service:** ask for a "3 things to know before you book a [service]" script.

## Workflow 16: Find Hashtags and Keywords

**Use it when:** you are not sure which hashtags or keywords will help local customers find you.

> **Time Saved:** About 25 minutes (from 30 down to 5).

```
Suggest hashtags for [business] in [town / region] on [platform].
Give me three groups: 5 local hashtags, 5 about what we sell, and
5 broader community or interest tags. Mark which are likely to be
very crowded. Then suggest 10 search keywords people might type to
find a business like mine nearby.
```

```result
Local: #Northbrook #NorthbrookEats #MillStreet ...
What we sell: #sourdough #celebrationcakes ... (#sourdough is very
crowded; use with smaller tags)
Search keywords: "bakery near me open Sunday", "custom birthday cake
Northbrook", ...
```

**Make it better:** check each suggested hashtag on the platform before using it; Claude cannot see how popular a hashtag is today.

**Variations:**

- **Google Business Profile:** ask for a 750-character business description using the top keywords naturally.
- **Etsy or marketplace sellers:** ask for listing tags within the platform's limits.

## Workflow 17: Write a Social Media Bio

**Use it when:** your profile bio is vague, out of date or missing the one thing visitors need to know.

> **Time Saved:** About 25 minutes (from 30 down to 5).

```
Write five versions of a [platform] bio for [business], under [number]
characters. Each must say what we do, where, and what to do next
(order, book, visit). Our tone: [tone]. Include one version with a
line break layout and one with no emojis.
```

```result
1. Family bakery on Mill Street, Northbrook
   Sourdough, pastries & made-to-order cakes
   Tue-Sun, 7 a.m.-3 p.m.
   Order a cake below
2. Baking in Northbrook since 2016. Bread from 7, cakes to order.
   Tap to order.
```

**Make it better:** keep your bios consistent across platforms. Ask Claude to adapt your favorite for each platform's limit.

**Variations:**

- **Personal brand (coach, consultant):** ask for a bio that names who you help and the result you get them.
- **Seasonal update:** ask for a holiday version with opening hours and an order deadline.

## Workflow 18: Plan a Giveaway or Contest

**Use it when:** you want to grow your following with a giveaway that is simple, fair and within the rules.

> **Time Saved:** About 1 hour (from 75 minutes down to 15).

```
Help me plan a social media giveaway for [business] on [platform].
Prize: [prize and value]. Goal: [followers, email sign-ups, local
awareness]. Give me: the entry method, the announcement post, a
short set of plain-English terms (who can enter, dates, how the
winner is chosen and contacted), a winner announcement, and a list
of things I must check in the platform's rules and my local laws.
```

```result
Entry: Follow us, like this post and tag a friend you'd share a cake
with. One entry per person. UK and US residents 18+...
Terms: Open from Monday 9 a.m. to Friday 5 p.m. Winner chosen at
random and contacted by direct message within 48 hours...
To check: the platform's promotion rules; whether your state or
country treats this as a lottery...
```

> **Check First:** Giveaway rules differ by platform and country. Read the platform's current promotion guidelines and check local law before you launch.

**Make it better:** ask for a follow-up post for people who did not win, with a small thank-you offer.

**Variations:**

- **Email list growth:** ask for an entry method through a sign-up form instead of tags.
- **Partner giveaway:** ask for a plan shared with a neighboring business, splitting the prize and the posting.

## Workflow 19: Learn From Your Post Results

**Use it when:** you have a month or two of posts and want to know what is actually working.

> **Time Saved:** About 1 hour (from 75 minutes down to 15).

```
Here are my last [number] posts with their results (reach, likes,
comments, shares, saves, link clicks). Please: 1) rank the top five
and bottom five; 2) say what the top posts have in common (topic,
format, time, caption style); 3) suggest five posts to try next
month based on what worked; 4) tell me what to stop doing.

[paste or attach a table of posts and numbers]
```

```result
Top posts have three things in common: a person in the first frame,
a story caption, and posting before 8 a.m.
Bottom posts: product-only photos with "available now" captions.
Stop: posting at 6 p.m.; your reach is a third of the morning.
Try: "Meet the team" series; "4 a.m. bake" videos; customer photos.
```

**Make it better:** export the numbers from your platform's insights page as a spreadsheet and attach it, so Claude reads the real figures.

**Variations:**

- **Email instead of social:** paste open and click rates per newsletter and ask the same questions.
- **Ads:** paste ad results and ask which audience and message gave the lowest cost per result.

## Workflow 20: Write Posts for a Slow Day or a Crisis

**Use it when:** something has gone wrong (a closure, a delay, bad weather) or you need to fill a quiet day fast.

> **Time Saved:** About 30 minutes (from 40 down to 10), when you have the least time.

```
Something has happened: [what, for example "our oven broke, we're
closed tomorrow" or "deliveries are delayed by the storm"]. Write a
short, calm, honest post for [platforms] that explains what
happened, what it means for customers, what we are doing about it,
and when we expect to be back to normal. Apologize once. Don't
overpromise. Then write a short follow-up post for when it's fixed.
```

```result
"Folks, our main oven gave up this morning. We're closed tomorrow
(Thursday) while it's repaired. Cake orders for this weekend are
safe - we've got a backup plan, and we'll message you directly if
anything changes. Sorry for the trouble. Back Friday at 7, with
extra cinnamon buns."
Follow-up: "The oven lives! Open as normal from 7 tomorrow..."
```

**Make it better:** keep a few of these ready-written in your Project for common problems, so you can post within minutes.

**Variations:**

- **Quiet day boost:** ask for a "today only" post that gives people a reason to come in this afternoon.
- **Serious incident:** for anything involving safety, injury or the law, talk to a professional before posting.
