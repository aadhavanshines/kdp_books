# Part II: The 100 Workflows

# Chapter 5: Marketing

Marketing is the job most small business owners know they should do more of and never have time for. These ten workflows cover the essentials: knowing your customer, saying what you do clearly, and keeping in touch with the people who already like you.

## Workflow 1: Describe Your Ideal Customer

**Use it when:** you are about to write any marketing and want it aimed at real people, not "everyone".

> **Time Saved:** About 1 hour (from 75 minutes down to 15).

```
I run [type of business] in [town]. Here is what I know about my
best customers: [who they are, what they buy, when, why they choose
us, what they complain about]. Please write two short customer
profiles (personas), each with: a name and one-line description,
what they want, what worries them, where they spend time online and
offline, and the words they would use to describe their problem.
Finish with three marketing messages that would speak to each one.
```

```result
1. "WEEKDAY WENDY" - office worker, 30s, buys breakfast on the way
to work.
Wants: something fresh and quick, no queue.
Worries: being late; paying too much for a coffee and pastry.
Where: Instagram on her commute; the station; the gym.
Her words: "I just need something decent before my 9 o'clock."
Messages:
- "In and out in two minutes. Fresh out of the oven since 4 a.m."
- "Breakfast deal: coffee and a pastry, $6, every weekday."
- "Order ahead on your phone. We'll have it waiting."
```

**Make it better:** paste ten real customer reviews and ask Claude to use their exact words in the profiles. Customers' own phrases make the best marketing copy.

**Variations:**

- **Service business:** describe clients by the problem they call you about ("burst pipe at 7 a.m.") rather than by what they buy.
- **Online shop:** add "what they search for" and "what makes them abandon a cart" to the profile.

## Workflow 2: Write Your One-Sentence Pitch

**Use it when:** you need a clear answer to "So, what do you do?" for your website, social profiles and networking.

> **Time Saved:** About 45 minutes (from 1 hour down to 15).

```
Help me write a one-sentence description of my business. We are
[type of business] for [who]. We help them [result]. What makes us
different: [one or two things]. Give me ten options under 20 words
each: three plain, three warm, two bold, two playful. Avoid jargon
and the words "solutions", "passionate" and "world-class".
```

```result
Plain: "Pine Ridge Plumbing fixes leaks, boilers and blocked drains
across the valley, usually the same day."
Warm: "Your friendly local plumber: on time, tidy, and honest about
the price before we start."
Bold: "Same-day plumbing, fixed prices, no mess. That's a promise."
Playful: "We'll handle the drips so you don't have to."
```

**Make it better:** pick your two favorites and ask Claude to test them: "Which is clearer to someone who has never heard of us, and why?"

**Variations:**

- **For a networking event:** ask for a 30-second spoken version that ends with a question.
- **For a social bio:** ask for versions under 150 characters, with one emoji at most.

## Workflow 3: Plan a Month of Marketing

**Use it when:** marketing keeps slipping because you never decided what to do this month.

> **Time Saved:** About 1.5 hours (from 2 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Please plan one month of simple marketing for [type of business]
in [month]. My goals: [for example, more weekday customers, sell
gift cards]. My budget: [amount]. Time I can spend: [hours per
week]. Channels I use: [email, Instagram, Facebook, flyers, local
events...]. Include seasonal dates and local events in [month] that
fit my business. Give me a week-by-week table: what to do, on which
channel, and roughly how long it will take.
```

```result
| Week | What to do | Channel | Time |
| 1 | Launch "Bake-Off Season" pre-order for holiday cakes | Email, Instagram | 1 h |
| 2 | Behind-the-scenes video: 4 a.m. bake | Instagram, Facebook | 45 min |
| 3 | Gift card push before payday | Email, in-store sign | 1 h |
| 4 | Thank-you post and customer photo round-up | Instagram | 30 min |
```

**Make it better:** ask "Which one of these would make the biggest difference if I only did one?" Then do that one well.

**Variations:**

- **Very busy month:** say "I have only one hour a week" and ask for the three highest-impact actions.
- **Launching something new:** ask Claude to build the month around the launch date, with a teaser, the launch and a follow-up.

> **Check First:** Claude may suggest local events or dates from general knowledge. Confirm event dates locally before you plan around them.

## Workflow 4: Write a Monthly Email Newsletter

**Use it when:** you have an email list and want to keep in touch without spending an afternoon on it.

> **Time Saved:** About 70 minutes (from 90 down to 20).

```
Write this month's email newsletter for [business name]. Our tone:
[tone]. News to include: [new products, events, staff news, offers].
Keep it under 250 words, with a subject line, a short preview line,
three short sections with headings, and one clear call to action:
[what you want readers to do]. Write three subject line options.
```

```result
Subject options:
1. Pumpkin season is here (and so is our new loaf)
2. Your October bake list
3. Something new is rising at Northbrook

Preview: A new seeded sourdough, Saturday baking class, and a little
thank-you.

NEW ON THE SHELF
Our seeded rye sourdough lands Friday. Crunchy crust, soft middle,
and a lot of seeds...

CALL TO ACTION: Book your place at Saturday's class - 8 spots left.
```

**Make it better:** paste last month's newsletter and its open rate, and ask what to change to get more people to open and click.

**Variations:**

- **No news this month?** Ask for a newsletter built around a tip, a behind-the-scenes story and a customer question.
- **B2B business:** ask for a more businesslike tone and one short case study instead of offers.

## Workflow 5: Write Product or Service Descriptions

**Use it when:** your website, menu or online shop has thin or missing descriptions.

> **Time Saved:** About 2 hours for ten products (from 2.5 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Write product descriptions for my [online shop / menu / services
page]. For each item below, write a 50-word description that
explains what it is, who it is for and why they will love it, in
our tone: [tone]. Then a 10-word version for listings. Do not
invent facts: only use the details I give you.

[list each product with its key facts: size, materials,
ingredients, price, what makes it special]
```

```result
SEEDED RYE SOURDOUGH (800 g, $8.50)
Long version: Our slow-proved rye, packed with sunflower, pumpkin
and flax seeds. A deep, nutty flavor and a crust that crackles when
you slice it. Perfect with soup, cheese, or just butter. Baked
fresh every morning from Friday to Sunday.
Short version: Nutty, seedy, crackly-crusted rye. Fresh Friday to
Sunday.
```

**Make it better:** for an online shop, ask Claude to add three search phrases a customer might type to find each product.

**Variations:**

- **Services:** describe each service by the problem it solves, what is included, how long it takes and the price.
- **Marketplace listings:** give the platform's character limits and ask Claude to stay under them.

> **Check First:** Check every claim about ingredients, allergens, materials or sizes against the real product. Claude must never be your source for allergy information.

## Workflow 6: Create a Promotion or Offer

**Use it when:** you want to run a sale or offer but are not sure what would work without hurting your margins.

> **Time Saved:** About 1 hour (from 75 minutes down to 15).

```
I want to run a promotion for [business]. Goal: [more first-time
customers / clear stock / fill quiet days]. My average sale is
[amount] and my rough profit margin is [percentage]. Suggest five
promotion ideas, from least to most expensive for me. For each:
how it works, the likely cost to me per sale, the risk, and a
one-line announcement. Do not suggest discounts over [maximum
discount]%.
```

```result
1. "Quiet Tuesday" loyalty stamp: double stamps on Tuesdays.
   Cost: about $0.40 per visit. Risk: low.
   Announcement: "Tuesdays just got sweeter: double stamps all day."
2. Free mini pastry with any loaf before 9 a.m.
   Cost: about $0.60. Risk: regulars expect it forever.
   ...
```

**Make it better:** once you choose, ask Claude to write the sign for the counter, the social post and the email, all matching.

**Variations:**

- **Service business:** ask for promotions that fill quiet appointment slots rather than discounting your busiest days.
- **Clearing old stock:** give quantities and ask for a bundle or "last chance" idea that protects your brand.

## Workflow 7: Turn Customer Reviews Into Marketing Insights

**Use it when:** you have reviews on Google, social media or your shop, but have never sat down to learn from them.

> **Time Saved:** About 1.5 hours (from 2 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Below are [number] reviews of my business. Please: 1) list the five
things customers praise most, with how often each comes up; 2) list
the complaints, with how often; 3) quote the five most vivid
phrases customers use (word for word); 4) suggest three marketing
messages based only on what customers actually say. Do not make up
any review.

[paste reviews, with names removed]
```

```result
Most praised: friendliness of staff (14 of 40), the sourdough (11),
the smell when you walk in (6)...
Complaints: weekend queues (7), parking (3).
Customer phrases: "like walking into a warm hug", "the best
cinnamon bun I've had outside Sweden"...
Message idea: "Like walking into a warm hug - and that's just the
smell."
```

**Make it better:** use the complaints list too. Ask Claude for three practical fixes for your most common complaint.

**Variations:**

- **Few reviews?** Paste customer emails or survey answers instead, with names removed.
- **Competitor research:** paste public reviews of two competitors and ask what customers wish they did better.

> **Check First:** You may quote real reviews in your marketing only where the platform's rules and the reviewer allow it. Never invent or edit reviews.

## Workflow 8: Write a Press Release for Local News

**Use it when:** you have real news, such as an opening, an anniversary, an award or a charity event, and want local papers or radio to cover it.

> **Time Saved:** About 1.5 hours (from 2 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Write a short press release for local media. News: [what is
happening, when, where]. Why it matters to the community: [reason].
A quote from me: [your rough words, which you can polish]. Contact:
[name, email, phone]. Use standard press release format, under 350
words, with a headline, a one-line summary, and the most important
facts in the first paragraph. Then write a three-sentence email to
send it to a local journalist.
```

```result
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE

Northbrook Bakery Turns Ten, Celebrates With Free Bread for Food Bank

Northbrook, October 2 - Northbrook Bakery will donate one loaf to
the Northbrook Food Bank for every loaf sold on Saturday, October 12,
to mark ten years on Mill Street...
```

**Make it better:** ask Claude for a list of the kinds of local outlets that cover this sort of story (newspaper, radio, community newsletters), then find the real contacts yourself.

**Variations:**

- **Award or milestone:** ask Claude to suggest a local angle, such as jobs created or a community link.
- **Trade press:** ask for a more technical version for an industry magazine or newsletter.

## Workflow 9: Write a Website Home Page

**Use it when:** your website's home page is out of date, vague, or was written years ago.

> **Time Saved:** About 2.5 hours (from 3 hours down to 30 minutes).

```
Write the text for my website's home page. Business: [details or
"see my business profile"]. Main customer: [who]. The one action I
want visitors to take: [call, book, buy, visit]. Include: a headline
under 10 words, a subheading, three short benefit sections with
headings, a short "about us" paragraph, three customer questions
with answers, and a call-to-action button label. Plain, friendly
English. Mark any place where I need to add a real photo, review or
detail.
```

```result
HEADLINE: Fresh bread, baked here since 4 a.m.
SUBHEADING: A family bakery on Mill Street. Sourdough, pastries and
celebration cakes, made by hand every day.

BENEFIT 1 - Baked on site, every morning
No frozen dough, no shortcuts...
[ADD PHOTO: the morning bake]
BUTTON: See today's menu
```

**Make it better:** paste your current home page and ask "What would confuse a first-time visitor?" before rewriting it.

**Variations:**

- **About page:** ask for a 200-word founder story built from five facts you provide.
- **Landing page for one offer:** give the offer, deadline and price, and ask for a single-purpose page.

## Workflow 10: Check Your Marketing Before It Goes Out

**Use it when:** you have written a flyer, ad, email or post yourself and want a quick second opinion.

> **Time Saved:** About 20 minutes per piece (from 30 down to 10), and fewer embarrassing typos.

```
Please review the marketing text below as a sharp, friendly editor.
Check: spelling and grammar; whether the main message is clear in
the first line; whether the call to action is obvious; anything
that could be misread or sounds like an exaggeration; and whether
it fits this audience: [who]. List the problems first, then give me
an improved version that keeps my voice.

[paste your text]
```

```result
Problems:
1. The offer ("20% off") is in the last line; most readers won't
   get there. Move it up.
2. "Best cakes in the state" is a claim you can't prove. Try
   "our most-ordered cake".
3. Typo: "recieve".
Improved version: ...
```

**Make it better:** for anything printed, ask Claude to check the text once more as if it were the final proof, looking only for typos, dates, prices and phone numbers.

**Variations:**

- **Ads:** add the ad platform's character limits and ask for three tighter versions.
- **Translations:** ask Claude to check a translated flyer for meaning, then have a native speaker confirm it.
