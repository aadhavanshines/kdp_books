# Claude AI for Small Business: Prompt Library

Every prompt from the book, in order. Replace each [bracket] with your own details.

## Marketing

### Workflow 1: Describe Your Ideal Customer

*Use it when:* you are about to write any marketing and want it aimed at real people, not "everyone".

```
I run [type of business] in [town]. Here is what I know about my best customers: [who they are, what they buy, when, why they choose us, what they complain about]. Please write two short customer profiles (personas), each with: a name and one-line description, what they want, what worries them, where they spend time online and offline, and the words they would use to describe their problem. Finish with three marketing messages that would speak to each one.
```

*Make it better:* paste ten real customer reviews and ask Claude to use their exact words in the profiles. Customers' own phrases make the best marketing copy.

### Workflow 2: Write Your One-Sentence Pitch

*Use it when:* you need a clear answer to "So, what do you do?" for your website, social profiles and networking.

```
Help me write a one-sentence description of my business. We are [type of business] for [who]. We help them [result]. What makes us different: [one or two things]. Give me ten options under 20 words each: three plain, three warm, two bold, two playful. Avoid jargon and the words "solutions", "passionate" and "world-class".
```

*Make it better:* pick your two favorites and ask Claude to test them: "Which is clearer to someone who has never heard of us, and why?"

### Workflow 3: Plan a Month of Marketing

*Use it when:* marketing keeps slipping because you never decided what to do this month.

```
Please plan one month of simple marketing for [type of business] in [month]. My goals: [for example, more weekday customers, sell gift cards]. My budget: [amount]. Time I can spend: [hours per week]. Channels I use: [email, Instagram, Facebook, flyers, local events...]. Include seasonal dates and local events in [month] that fit my business. Give me a week-by-week table: what to do, on which channel, and roughly how long it will take.
```

*Make it better:* ask "Which one of these would make the biggest difference if I only did one?" Then do that one well.

### Workflow 4: Write a Monthly Email Newsletter

*Use it when:* you have an email list and want to keep in touch without spending an afternoon on it.

```
Write this month's email newsletter for [business name]. Our tone: [tone]. News to include: [new products, events, staff news, offers]. Keep it under 250 words, with a subject line, a short preview line, three short sections with headings, and one clear call to action: [what you want readers to do]. Write three subject line options.
```

*Make it better:* paste last month's newsletter and its open rate, and ask what to change to get more people to open and click.

### Workflow 5: Write Product or Service Descriptions

*Use it when:* your website, menu or online shop has thin or missing descriptions.

```
Write product descriptions for my [online shop / menu / services page]. For each item below, write a 50-word description that explains what it is, who it is for and why they will love it, in our tone: [tone]. Then a 10-word version for listings. Do not invent facts: only use the details I give you.

[list each product with its key facts: size, materials, ingredients, price, what makes it special]
```

*Make it better:* for an online shop, ask Claude to add three search phrases a customer might type to find each product.

### Workflow 6: Create a Promotion or Offer

*Use it when:* you want to run a sale or offer but are not sure what would work without hurting your margins.

```
I want to run a promotion for [business]. Goal: [more first-time customers / clear stock / fill quiet days]. My average sale is [amount] and my rough profit margin is [percentage]. Suggest five promotion ideas, from least to most expensive for me. For each: how it works, the likely cost to me per sale, the risk, and a one-line announcement. Do not suggest discounts over [maximum discount]%.
```

*Make it better:* once you choose, ask Claude to write the sign for the counter, the social post and the email, all matching.

### Workflow 7: Turn Customer Reviews Into Marketing Insights

*Use it when:* you have reviews on Google, social media or your shop, but have never sat down to learn from them.

```
Below are [number] reviews of my business. Please: 1) list the five things customers praise most, with how often each comes up; 2) list the complaints, with how often; 3) quote the five most vivid phrases customers use (word for word); 4) suggest three marketing messages based only on what customers actually say. Do not make up any review.

[paste reviews, with names removed]
```

*Make it better:* use the complaints list too. Ask Claude for three practical fixes for your most common complaint.

### Workflow 8: Write a Press Release for Local News

*Use it when:* you have real news, such as an opening, an anniversary, an award or a charity event, and want local papers or radio to cover it.

```
Write a short press release for local media. News: [what is happening, when, where]. Why it matters to the community: [reason]. A quote from me: [your rough words, which you can polish]. Contact: [name, email, phone]. Use standard press release format, under 350 words, with a headline, a one-line summary, and the most important facts in the first paragraph. Then write a three-sentence email to send it to a local journalist.
```

*Make it better:* ask Claude for a list of the kinds of local outlets that cover this sort of story (newspaper, radio, community newsletters), then find the real contacts yourself.

### Workflow 9: Write a Website Home Page

*Use it when:* your website's home page is out of date, vague, or was written years ago.

```
Write the text for my website's home page. Business: [details or "see my business profile"]. Main customer: [who]. The one action I want visitors to take: [call, book, buy, visit]. Include: a headline under 10 words, a subheading, three short benefit sections with headings, a short "about us" paragraph, three customer questions with answers, and a call-to-action button label. Plain, friendly English. Mark any place where I need to add a real photo, review or detail.
```

*Make it better:* paste your current home page and ask "What would confuse a first-time visitor?" before rewriting it.

### Workflow 10: Check Your Marketing Before It Goes Out

*Use it when:* you have written a flyer, ad, email or post yourself and want a quick second opinion.

```
Please review the marketing text below as a sharp, friendly editor. Check: spelling and grammar; whether the main message is clear in the first line; whether the call to action is obvious; anything that could be misread or sounds like an exaggeration; and whether it fits this audience: [who]. List the problems first, then give me an improved version that keeps my voice.

[paste your text]
```

*Make it better:* for anything printed, ask Claude to check the text once more as if it were the final proof, looking only for typos, dates, prices and phone numbers.

## Social Media

### Workflow 11: Plan a Month of Posts

*Use it when:* you post when you remember, and want a simple plan you can actually stick to.

```
Plan a month of social media posts for [business] on [platforms]. I can post [number] times a week. Mix: 40% behind the scenes, 30% products or services, 20% customers and community, 10% offers. Include dates that matter for my business in [month]. Give me a table: date, platform, post idea, what photo or video I need, and a one-line hook. Keep every idea doable with a phone in 10 minutes.
```

*Make it better:* ask Claude to turn the plan into a checklist you can check off, with the photo list grouped so you can shoot a week's photos in one go.

### Workflow 12: Write Captions From a Photo

*Use it when:* you have a good photo and no idea what to say.

```
Here is a photo for a social post for [business] on [platform]. [attach the photo] It shows [describe it if needed]. Write three caption options in our voice: one short and punchy, one that tells a tiny story, and one that asks followers a question. Each under [number] words, with a clear call to action and up to [number] relevant hashtags.
```

*Make it better:* keep your best-performing captions in your Project's files, and ask Claude to write new ones "in the style of these".

### Workflow 13: Repurpose One Piece of Content Five Ways

*Use it when:* you have written something good once, like a newsletter, a blog post or a talk, and want more mileage from it.

```
Below is a [newsletter / blog post / talk transcript]. Turn it into: 1) an Instagram caption, 2) a Facebook post, 3) a LinkedIn post, 4) three short tips for a carousel (one sentence each, plus a cover title), 5) a 30-second script for a talking-to-camera video. Keep our voice and the key message: [main point]. Don't add facts that aren't in the original.

[paste the content]
```

*Make it better:* ask Claude to space the five pieces over two weeks, with the strongest one first.

### Workflow 14: Reply to Comments and Messages

*Use it when:* comments and direct messages pile up and you want friendly, consistent replies.

```
Here are [number] comments and messages from our social media. Draft a short reply to each in our voice: friendly, human, under 40 words. Answer questions using only these facts: [hours, prices, links, policies]. If a comment is a complaint, reply politely and invite them to message us privately. If a comment is abusive or spam, write "SKIP" and say why.

[paste comments, with usernames replaced by numbers]
```

*Make it better:* add your FAQ sheet (bonus template 3) to your Project so every reply uses your real answers.

### Workflow 15: Write Short Video Scripts

*Use it when:* you know video gets more reach but freeze when the camera starts.

```
Write three scripts for 30-second vertical videos for [business]. Topic ideas: [topics or "suggest some"]. Each script: a hook in the first 3 seconds, three short beats, and a call to action. Show what to film for each line in brackets. Natural spoken English, as if talking to a friend. No more than 75 words of speech each.
```

*Make it better:* ask for on-screen text for each beat, for people watching with the sound off.

### Workflow 16: Find Hashtags and Keywords

*Use it when:* you are not sure which hashtags or keywords will help local customers find you.

```
Suggest hashtags for [business] in [town / region] on [platform]. Give me three groups: 5 local hashtags, 5 about what we sell, and 5 broader community or interest tags. Mark which are likely to be very crowded. Then suggest 10 search keywords people might type to find a business like mine nearby.
```

*Make it better:* check each suggested hashtag on the platform before using it; Claude cannot see how popular a hashtag is today.

### Workflow 17: Write a Social Media Bio

*Use it when:* your profile bio is vague, out of date or missing the one thing visitors need to know.

```
Write five versions of a [platform] bio for [business], under [number] characters. Each must say what we do, where, and what to do next (order, book, visit). Our tone: [tone]. Include one version with a line break layout and one with no emojis.
```

*Make it better:* keep your bios consistent across platforms. Ask Claude to adapt your favorite for each platform's limit.

### Workflow 18: Plan a Giveaway or Contest

*Use it when:* you want to grow your following with a giveaway that is simple, fair and within the rules.

```
Help me plan a social media giveaway for [business] on [platform]. Prize: [prize and value]. Goal: [followers, email sign-ups, local awareness]. Give me: the entry method, the announcement post, a short set of plain-English terms (who can enter, dates, how the winner is chosen and contacted), a winner announcement, and a list of things I must check in the platform's rules and my local laws.
```

*Make it better:* ask for a follow-up post for people who did not win, with a small thank-you offer.

### Workflow 19: Learn From Your Post Results

*Use it when:* you have a month or two of posts and want to know what is actually working.

```
Here are my last [number] posts with their results (reach, likes, comments, shares, saves, link clicks). Please: 1) rank the top five and bottom five; 2) say what the top posts have in common (topic, format, time, caption style); 3) suggest five posts to try next month based on what worked; 4) tell me what to stop doing.

[paste or attach a table of posts and numbers]
```

*Make it better:* export the numbers from your platform's insights page as a spreadsheet and attach it, so Claude reads the real figures.

### Workflow 20: Write Posts for a Slow Day or a Crisis

*Use it when:* something has gone wrong (a closure, a delay, bad weather) or you need to fill a quiet day fast.

```
Something has happened: [what, for example "our oven broke, we're closed tomorrow" or "deliveries are delayed by the storm"]. Write a short, calm, honest post for [platforms] that explains what happened, what it means for customers, what we are doing about it, and when we expect to be back to normal. Apologize once. Don't overpromise. Then write a short follow-up post for when it's fixed.
```

*Make it better:* keep a few of these ready-written in your Project for common problems, so you can post within minutes.

## Sales Emails and Follow-Ups

### Workflow 21: Write a Cold Email to a Potential Client

*Use it when:* you want to approach a business or person you have not worked with before.

```
Write a short first email from me to [name and role] at [their business]. What I know about them: [details, for example they just opened a second shop]. What I offer: [service]. Why it might help them: [reason]. Ask for one small next step: [a 15-minute call, a sample, a visit]. Under 120 words, friendly, no hype, no "I hope this email finds you well". Give three subject lines.
```

*Make it better:* ask Claude for a version that is 30% shorter. Shorter first emails usually get more replies.

### Workflow 22: Follow Up When There's No Reply

*Use it when:* you sent a quote or proposal a week ago and heard nothing.

```
I sent [what] to [who] on [date] and haven't heard back. Write three follow-up emails: one for 3 days later, one for 10 days later, and a final polite "closing the loop" email for 3 weeks later. Each under 70 words, friendly, adding something useful each time (a tip, a deadline, a different option). Never guilt-trip.
```

*Make it better:* save this sequence in your Project, and use it for every quote you send.

### Workflow 23: Write a Quote Cover Email

*Use it when:* you are sending a price and want the email to sell the value, not just state a number.

```
Write the email to go with my quote for [job] for [client]. Price: [amount], valid until [date]. What's included: [list]. What's not included: [list]. Why us: [two reasons]. Next step: [how to accept]. Under 180 words. Make the price easy to understand and the next step obvious.
```

*Make it better:* ask Claude to list three questions the client might have about the quote, and add short answers to the email.

### Workflow 24: Win Back Past Customers

*Use it when:* you have customers who used to buy and have gone quiet.

```
Write a short "we miss you" email for customers who haven't bought from [business] in [time]. Mention what's new since they last visited: [news]. Include one small, time-limited reason to come back: [offer]. Warm, not desperate, under 120 words. Give three subject lines, and a version for text message under 160 characters.
```

*Make it better:* ask for a second email for customers who don't respond, with a different angle.

### Workflow 25: Ask for a Referral

*Use it when:* a happy customer would probably recommend you, if you asked nicely.

```
Write a short, natural email asking [customer] for a referral. We just [finished job / delivered order] and they said [what they said]. Ask if they know anyone else who might need [service]. Offer [referral thank-you, if any]. Make it easy to say no. Under 100 words.
```

*Make it better:* ask Claude to also write a short message the customer can forward, so referring you takes them ten seconds.

### Workflow 26: Chase a Late Payment

*Use it when:* an invoice is overdue and you dread writing the email.

```
Write three emails to chase an overdue invoice. Client: [name]. Invoice: [number], amount [amount], due [date]. Email 1: friendly reminder (assume they forgot). Email 2: one week later, firmer, asking for a payment date. Email 3: two weeks later, final notice stating [your next step, for example late fees as in our terms, or pausing work]. Polite and professional throughout. No threats I can't carry out.
```

*Make it better:* add a line with a payment link or bank details placeholder, so paying is one click.

### Workflow 27: Announce a Price Increase

*Use it when:* your costs have gone up and you need to raise prices without losing good customers.

```
Write an email telling customers that our prices will rise by [amount or percentage] from [date]. Reason: [honest reason]. What stays the same: [quality, service, etc.]. Anything we're doing to soften it: [for example, current prices until date, loyalty discount]. Honest, brief, no long apology. Under 150 words. Then write a short version for a sign or a social post.
```

*Make it better:* ask Claude to list the three objections customers might raise, with short, calm answers for your staff.

### Workflow 28: Thank a New Customer

*Use it when:* someone has just bought for the first time and you want them to come back.

```
Write a short welcome email for new customers of [business] after their first [purchase / booking]. Thank them, tell them one useful thing to get the most from what they bought: [tip], invite them to [next step: follow us, join the list, book again], and tell them how to reach us. Under 120 words, warm, no hard sell.
```

*Make it better:* ask for a second email to send a week later, asking how it went.

### Workflow 29: Write a Sales Page for One Product or Service

*Use it when:* you want a single page or long email that sells one thing well.

```
Write a sales page for [product or service]. Who it's for: [customer]. Their problem: [problem]. What they get: [features, each with its benefit]. Price: [price]. Proof: [real reviews or results, if any]. Common doubts: [doubts]. Structure: headline, the problem, the solution, what's included, proof, answers to doubts, price and guarantee [if any], call to action. Plain English, no hype, no invented claims. Mark places where I should add a real photo or quote.
```

*Make it better:* ask Claude to read the page as a doubtful customer and list what would stop them from buying.

### Workflow 30: Prepare for a Sales Call

*Use it when:* you have an important call or meeting with a potential client.

```
I have a sales call with [who] about [what]. What I know: [notes]. Help me prepare: 1) five good questions to understand their needs; 2) the three objections they are most likely to raise, with calm answers; 3) how to explain my price in one sentence; 4) a clear next step to propose at the end. Keep it on one page.
```

*Make it better:* after the call, paste your notes and use Workflow 22 or Workflow 84 to send the follow-up within the hour.

## Customer Service

### Workflow 31: Answer a Customer Email

*Use it when:* a customer has asked a question by email or message.

```
Draft a reply to this customer message. Use only the facts in my FAQ sheet and reply rules. If the answer isn't there, say so and leave a [CHECK] note for me instead of guessing. Warm, clear, under 120 words, signed [name].

[paste the customer's message]
```

*Make it better:* paste five emails at once and ask for five replies. Batch replying is far faster than one at a time.

### Workflow 32: Handle a Complaint

*Use it when:* a customer is unhappy and you want to put it right without making it worse.

```
A customer has complained. Here's their message and what actually happened: [your side, honestly]. Draft a reply that: thanks them, apologizes once for what we got wrong (and only that), explains briefly without blaming, offers [what we can offer under our reply rules], and says what we'll do to stop it happening again. Calm, human, under 150 words. Don't admit legal fault.

[paste the complaint]
```

*Make it better:* before sending, ask Claude "How might this customer read this reply? Is anything likely to annoy them?"

### Workflow 33: Reply to Online Reviews

*Use it when:* you have new public reviews, good or bad, to answer.

```
Write public replies to these reviews of [business]. Positive reviews: thank them by first name, mention something specific they said, under 50 words. Negative reviews: thank them, apologize briefly for their experience, don't argue in public, give a way to contact us directly, under 70 words. Never reveal private details about the customer.

[paste reviews]
```

*Make it better:* ask Claude for three different openings for positive replies, so your responses don't all start the same way.

### Workflow 34: Build an FAQ Page

*Use it when:* customers keep asking the same questions and your website doesn't answer them.

```
Below are customer questions from the last few months. Group them into topics, remove duplicates, and write a website FAQ page with clear, short answers using only the facts I give. Order the topics by how often they come up. Where I haven't given an answer, write [ANSWER NEEDED] instead of guessing.

Questions: [paste emails, messages, notes] Facts: [hours, prices, policies, delivery areas, etc.]
```

*Make it better:* use the finished page as the FAQ sheet in your Project, so Claude's replies always match your website.

### Workflow 35: Write a Returns or Refund Policy

*Use it when:* your policy is missing, unclear or causing arguments.

```
Help me write a clear returns and refund policy for [business] in [country / state]. We sell [what], [online / in store / both]. What I want: [your rules, for example 14 days for unused items, no refunds on custom orders]. Write it in plain English with headings, under 300 words. Then list anything I should check against consumer law where I trade.
```

*Make it better:* ask for a three-sentence summary to show at checkout or on receipts.

### Workflow 36: Build a Library of Saved Replies

*Use it when:* you and your team type the same answers over and over.

```
Using my FAQ sheet and reply rules, write a library of 15 saved replies for our most common customer messages: [list the types, for example order status, delivery areas, booking changes, gift cards]. Each: a short title, a reply under 80 words with [placeholders] for details, and a note on when NOT to use it.
```

*Make it better:* paste the library into your email program's templates or canned responses feature, so anyone can use it.

### Workflow 37: Calm Down an Angry Message

*Use it when:* you have received a rude or furious message and need to reply professionally.

```
I received the angry message below. First, tell me in two or three bullet points what the customer actually wants, ignoring the tone. Then draft a calm, short reply that addresses those points, sets a polite boundary if they were abusive, and suggests a next step. Don't match their tone and don't apologize for things we didn't do.

[paste the message]
```

*Make it better:* if you're upset, wait an hour before sending, even if the draft is ready.

### Workflow 38: Ask Happy Customers for Reviews

*Use it when:* you have happy customers but few online reviews.

```
Write a short message asking a happy customer to leave a review of [business] on [platform]. Send it [when, for example, the day after their visit]. Thank them, make it easy (include [review link]), suggest they mention [what they liked] only if they want to, and make it fine to ignore. Write an email version and a text version under 160 characters.
```

*Make it better:* put the review link on your receipts and in your email signature too.

### Workflow 39: Write a Customer Feedback Survey

*Use it when:* you want to learn what customers think, without a 30-question form nobody finishes.

```
Write a short customer feedback survey for [business]. What I want to learn: [goals, for example why people choose us, what would make them visit more often]. Maximum 6 questions, mostly multiple choice or rating scales, with one open question. Neutral wording that doesn't lead the answer. Then write a two-sentence invitation to take it.
```

*Make it better:* when the answers come back, paste them in and use Workflow 7 to find the themes.

### Workflow 40: Sort Your Inbox

*Use it when:* you open your inbox and don't know where to start.

```
Below are the subject lines and first lines of my unread emails. Sort them into: 1) urgent - reply today; 2) customers waiting for an answer; 3) money (invoices, payments, bills); 4) can wait this week; 5) newsletters and probable spam. For groups 1 and 2, suggest a one-line reply for each.

[paste subjects and first lines, with personal details removed]
```

*Make it better:* if your plan supports email connectors (Chapter 16), Claude can read your inbox directly; start with read-only access and draft replies, never auto-send.

## Admin and Operations

### Workflow 41: Write a Standard Operating Procedure

*Use it when:* a job is done differently every time, or only you know how to do it.

```
Turn my rough description of how we [do the task] into a clear standard operating procedure. Include: purpose, who does it, when, what you need, numbered steps (one action each), common mistakes, and how to check it was done right. Simple words a new hire would understand. Here's how I do it:

[describe it, or paste a voice-note transcript]
```

*Make it better:* record yourself explaining the task while you do it, use the phone app's voice input or a transcript, and paste that in.

### Workflow 42: Make an Opening and Closing Checklist

*Use it when:* small things get forgotten at the start or end of the day.

```
Write opening and closing checklists for [business]. Things that must happen: [list everything you can think of, in any order]. Group them by area, put them in a sensible order, and add anything a business like mine commonly forgets (safety, security, cash, equipment). Each list must fit on one printed page with check boxes.
```

*Make it better:* after a month, ask Claude to add anything that went wrong because it wasn't on the list.

### Workflow 43: Email a Supplier

*Use it when:* you need to order, query a delivery, ask for a better price or raise a problem with a supplier.

```
Write an email to my supplier [name] about [order / problem / price]. Facts: [order number, dates, quantities, what went wrong]. What I want: [outcome]. Tone: [friendly and firm / just friendly]. Under 130 words, with a clear request and a date to reply by.
```

*Make it better:* keep a short note of each supplier's quirks in your Project ("Paula prefers phone calls for urgent issues").

### Workflow 44: Plan a Staff Schedule

*Use it when:* building the weekly schedule takes an hour of juggling.

```
Help me build next week's staff schedule. Opening hours: [hours]. Staff, their contracted hours and availability: [list]. Rules: [minimum staff per shift, breaks, who can open or close, max hours]. Busy times: [when]. Give me a table by day and shift, the hours each person gets, and any rule you couldn't meet.
```

*Make it better:* keep each person's availability in a spreadsheet and attach it each week, instead of retyping it.

### Workflow 45: Write Clear Instructions for Customers

*Use it when:* customers need to follow steps: aftercare, setup, preparation for an appointment, how to use a product.

```
Write simple instructions for customers on how to [task]. Who's reading: [customer type]. What they need to know: [steps, warnings, tips]. Numbered steps, one action per step, short sentences. Add a "common questions" section with three questions. Under 300 words.
```

*Make it better:* ask for a version with larger text and fewer words, to print on a card that goes in the box.

### Workflow 46: Fill In and Understand Forms

*Use it when:* a long form, application or official letter needs filling in and you don't know what half the questions mean.

```
I need to complete the form below for [purpose]. Please: 1) explain in plain English what each section is asking for; 2) list the documents and information I need to gather; 3) flag any section where a mistake could be costly or where I should get advice. Don't fill in personal or financial details for me.

[paste the form text or attach it, with personal details removed]
```

*Make it better:* ask Claude to turn the document list into a checklist with a date to gather each item.

### Workflow 47: Organize a Messy List

*Use it when:* you have a jumble of notes, tasks, ideas or contacts and need order.

```
Here is a messy list of [tasks / notes / ideas / stock items]. Remove duplicates, group similar items, put each group in a sensible order, and mark anything that looks urgent or unclear. Output a clean list with headings.

[paste the list]
```

*Make it better:* take a photo of a whiteboard or handwritten list with the phone app and ask Claude to read it.

### Workflow 48: Plan an Event

*Use it when:* you are running an open day, launch, class, pop-up or workshop.

```
Help me plan [event] on [date] at [place] for about [number] people. Budget: [amount]. Goal: [goal]. Give me: a timeline of tasks from now until the day, a run-of-show for the day itself (time, what happens, who's responsible), a shopping and equipment list, a simple budget table, and three ways to promote it.
```

*Make it better:* ask Claude for a "what could go wrong" list with a backup plan for each.

### Workflow 49: Write a Business Email You've Been Putting Off

*Use it when:* there is one email you have avoided for days because it is awkward.

```
I need to write an email I've been putting off. Situation: [what's going on, honestly]. Who it's to: [relationship]. What I want to happen: [outcome]. What I'm worried about: [worry]. Draft it in a clear, kind and direct tone, under 150 words. Then give me one softer and one firmer version.
```

*Make it better:* read it aloud before sending. If it doesn't sound like you, ask Claude to use your own wording from a past email.

### Workflow 50: Write a Simple Contingency Plan

*Use it when:* you realize that if one thing broke (power, a key person, a supplier), you would have no plan.

```
Help me write a simple contingency plan for [business]. Things that would hurt us most: [for example key supplier fails, owner is ill, power cut, website down, flood]. For each: early warning signs, what to do in the first hour and the first week, who to contact, and one thing to put in place now. Keep it to two pages.
```

*Make it better:* print it, put it somewhere everyone knows, and review it every six months with Workflow 90.

## Bookkeeping Prep

### Workflow 51: Categorize Your Expenses

*Use it when:* you have a month of bank or card transactions to sort into categories.

```
Here are my business transactions for [month], exported from my bank (account numbers removed). Put each one into one of these categories: [your categories, for example Ingredients, Packaging, Rent, Utilities, Equipment, Marketing, Fees, Travel, Owner drawings, Other]. Return a table: date, description, amount, category, and a "?" for anything you're unsure about. Then total each category. Show your totals so I can check them.

[paste or attach the transactions]
```
