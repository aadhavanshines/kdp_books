# Part I: Getting Started

# Chapter 1: What an AI Assistant Is (and Isn't)

![A grandmother and grandfather at the kitchen table, smiling as they read an answer on a tablet.](images/ill-couple-tablet.jpg)

Let's start with the big question: what exactly is an AI assistant?

"AI" stands for **artificial intelligence**. It is a type of computer program that has learned from an enormous amount of written text: books, articles, websites, instructions and conversations. From all that reading, it has learned how language works and a great deal about the world.

An **AI assistant** is an app or a website where you can chat with one of these programs. You type (or speak) a message, and the assistant writes a reply. You can then answer back, ask a follow-up question, or ask it to change something. It works much like texting a very well-read friend.

## What It Feels Like

Imagine you write:

```text
What is a good gift for my grandson who is 12
and loves football?
```

A few seconds later, the assistant replies with a short list of ideas: a ball signed by his favorite team, a book about famous players, tickets to a local game, a training jersey with his name on the back. Then you can ask for more:

```text
He already has a lot of football gear.
Something under $30 that isn't clothing?
```

And it gives you new ideas that fit. That back-and-forth is the whole secret. You do not need special commands or technical words. You just talk.

## What an AI Assistant Is Good At

AI assistants are especially good at:

- **Writing:** letters, emails, cards, speeches, posts and short stories
- **Explaining:** turning complicated words into plain English
- **Planning:** trips, meals, weekly schedules and to-do lists
- **Ideas:** gifts, hobbies, recipes, games and conversation starters
- **Summarizing:** turning a long letter or article into a few key points
- **Practicing:** a new language, a job interview, or a hard conversation
- **Patience:** answering the same question as many times as you like

## What It Is Not

It is just as important to know what an AI assistant is *not*.

**It is not a person.** It does not have feelings, memories of you as a friend, or a life of its own, even though it may write in a friendly way. It is a very clever tool.

**It is not always right.** It sometimes states things confidently that are simply wrong. Experts call this "making things up." Chapter 14 shows you how to catch it.

**It is not a doctor, lawyer or financial adviser.** It can help you understand things and prepare good questions, but it cannot examine you, and it does not know your full situation.

**It is not a search engine, exactly.** A search engine shows you a list of websites. An AI assistant reads and writes an answer for you. Many assistants can now also look things up on the web, and some show you where they found the information.

**It does not know everything about today.** Some assistants can look up recent news; others only know what they learned up to a certain date. If you need today's weather, train times or opening hours, check the official source.

> **Remember:** Think of an AI assistant as a knowledgeable, tireless helper who is occasionally wrong. Use it for help and ideas. Check anything important.

## Where AI Already Shows Up in Your Life

You may already be using AI without knowing it:

- The voice assistant on your phone or smart speaker that sets timers and tells you the weather
- The suggested words that appear as you type a text message
- The way your email moves junk mail out of sight
- Captions that appear on videos

The AI assistants in this book go further: you can have a real conversation with them about almost anything.

## Is It Safe?

Using a well-known AI assistant is about as safe as using email or a shopping website, as long as you follow a few common-sense rules. The most important ones are:

1. **Never type passwords, bank details, or your Social Security or other ID numbers** into an AI chat.
2. **Only use well-known apps** from your phone's official app store, or the company's official website.
3. **Be suspicious of anyone who pressures you** about money, whether that's a person, an email, or a voice on the phone. AI has made some scams more convincing, and Chapter 12 shows you how to spot them.

You will find these rules repeated throughout the book, because they matter.

## Is It Free?

The major AI assistants offer a **free version** that is more than enough for everything in this book. Most also sell a paid plan with extra features, usually around the price of a streaming TV service each month. You do not need to pay for anything to get started. If an app asks you to upgrade, you can simply close the message.

> **Try It:** Before you set anything up, think of three things you would like help with this week. A letter you have been meaning to write? A recipe for leftover vegetables? A question about your new phone? Write them on the notes page at the back of this book. We will use them soon.

## Key Points

- An AI assistant is a program you can chat with in plain English.
- It is good at writing, explaining, planning and ideas.
- It is not a person, it can make mistakes, and it is no substitute for a professional.
- Free versions are enough to start.
- Never share passwords, bank details or ID numbers with it.
