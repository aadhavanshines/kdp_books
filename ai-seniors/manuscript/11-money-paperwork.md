# Chapter 11: Money, Bills and Paperwork

Official letters, bills and forms can be confusing even for experts. An AI assistant can help you understand them, organize your budget, and write clear replies. This chapter shows you how, and how to do it without sharing anything private.

> **Warning:** An AI assistant is not a financial adviser, accountant or lawyer. Use it to understand and organize. For important decisions about savings, investments, taxes, wills or legal matters, speak to a qualified professional.

## The Golden Privacy Rule for Paperwork

Before you paste anything from a letter or bill into an AI, **remove the private details**:

- Your full name and address
- Account numbers, policy numbers and customer numbers
- Your Social Security number or any ID number
- Your date of birth
- Card numbers, PINs and passwords

You can simply type the part you want explained, or replace the details with words like *[my account]*.

## Understanding a Confusing Letter

```text
I received a letter from my health insurance that says:
"Your claim has been processed. The provider's charges
exceeded the allowed amount, and the difference is
your responsibility as an out-of-network service."
What does this mean, in plain English? What should I
do next?
```

The AI will explain each part, and will often suggest sensible next steps, such as calling the insurer to ask about an appeal. Then you call the number printed on your letter.

## Understanding a Bill

```text
My electricity bill has a line called "delivery charge"
and another called "supply charge". What is the
difference? Why do I pay both?
```

```text
My phone bill went up by $15 this month. What are
common reasons for a sudden increase, and what should
I ask the phone company?
```

## A Simple Budget

```text
Please help me make a simple monthly budget. My income
is about $2,800 a month from my pension and Social
Security. My regular costs are: rent $1,100, utilities
$180, phone and internet $90, food $400, medicines $60,
car $250. Please show it as a table and tell me what
is left over.
```

You do not need to give real account details. Rounded amounts are enough.

```text
Please suggest ten simple ways a retired person living
alone can save money on everyday bills.
```

## Words You Don't Understand

```text
What is the difference between a Roth IRA and a
traditional IRA? Please explain in simple words, with
an example.
```

```text
What is a "required minimum distribution"? Does it
apply to me if I'm 75? I'll confirm with my accountant.
```

## Writing a Clear Reply

```text
Please write a short, polite letter to my bank asking
them to explain a $35 fee on my statement dated
March 3, and asking for it to be refunded because I
have never been charged it before. Leave blanks for
my name and account number.
```

## Forms

Forms can be long and full of official words. You can ask what a section means:

```text
I am filling in a form, and one question asks for my
"adjusted gross income". What is that, and where would
I find it?
```

> **Safety Tip:** Never type your real Social Security number, bank details or passwords into an AI chat, even to "check a form." Fill in those parts yourself, directly on the official form or website.

## Is This a Scam?

Many scams arrive looking like bills, refunds or official letters. If something seems off, AI can help you think it through. Copy the words of the message (without your personal details) and ask:

```text
I received this text message. Does it look like a scam?
What are the warning signs, and what should I do?

"USPS: Your package is on hold due to an unpaid
shipping fee of $1.99. Pay now to avoid return:
[link]"
```

The next chapter covers scams in detail.

> **Try It:** Find a non-private letter or leaflet you didn't fully understand: a warranty, a leaflet from your utility company, or the terms on a store card. Type one confusing sentence into the AI and ask it to explain.

## Key Points

- Remove names, numbers and private details before asking.
- Ask the AI to explain confusing letters and bills in plain English.
- Make a simple budget using rounded amounts.
- Get help writing clear, polite letters with blanks for your details.
- For big decisions, speak to a qualified professional.
