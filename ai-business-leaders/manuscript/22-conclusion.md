# Conclusion: The Leader's Job

AI will keep changing. The models you use next year will be better than the ones in this book, and the products will have different names. Some of today's vendors will be gone. Some of the use cases that scored "not yet" will become easy.

The leader's job will not change much. It is to keep asking the same plain questions:

- What business problem are we solving, and who owns it?
- What does it cost today, and how will we know if it improved?
- What does the whole thing cost, including the people?
- What could go wrong, and who catches it?
- Did it work? If not, are we willing to stop?

Companies that ask these questions consistently will get steady, compounding returns from AI. Companies that don't will keep buying licenses, running pilots and hoping.

Northfield Supply was not a technology company, and it didn't hire a team of AI experts. It started with a skeptical CFO, a customer service team drowning in email, and a CEO willing to use the tools herself. Within a year, it had a handful of use cases that paid back, a way of working that its finance team trusted, and a team that saw AI as a help rather than a threat. None of that needed a technical breakthrough. It needed management.

So start small. Measure honestly. Decide on evidence. Bring your people with you. Prove it, then scale it.

That is the whole method. The rest is practice.
