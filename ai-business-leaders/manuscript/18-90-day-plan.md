# Chapter 18: The 90-Day Plan

This chapter puts everything together into a plan you can start on Monday. It is designed for a mid-sized company starting more or less from scratch, and it takes you from "We need an AI plan" to a first proven pilot and a decision to scale in about 90 days.

The plan has three phases of roughly 30 days each: **Decide**, **Pilot** and **Prove**. A broad assistant rollout runs alongside.

Adjust the timing to your situation. A company with good data and an approved assistant already in place may move faster. A company with a major system upgrade underway may need longer. What matters is the order of the steps and the discipline of deciding on evidence.

## Before Day 1: Name the Owner

Appoint an AI program lead: one person who coordinates the plan, reports to a senior executive, and has enough time to do it, at least half their week for the first 90 days. In a mid-sized company, this is often a strong operations, finance or project manager, not necessarily a technologist. In a small company, it may be the owner.

## Phase 1: Decide (Days 1 to 30)

The aim of this phase is to choose your first use cases, approve the business case for the flagship, and set up the basics of governance.

**Week 1**

- Leadership team meeting: agree on goals for AI in the next year, using the questions from Chapters 1 and 2.
- Each leader starts their personal AI habit and log (Chapter 3).
- IT confirms or selects an approved general AI assistant.
- Form the working group: program lead, IT, a finance partner, and representatives from the departments most likely to be involved.

**Week 2**

- Run the opportunity map interviews and data requests (Chapter 4).
- Draft the one-page AI use policy (Chapter 15 and Appendix A).
- Identify your early AI champions: people already using AI on their own.

**Week 3**

- Score the candidates (Chapter 5) and choose a portfolio: a flagship, a quick win and a foundation.
- Start the quick win immediately if it needs only the approved assistant.
- For the flagship, start vendor research or confirm the route (Chapter 7).
- Publish the AI use policy and run the first everyone-level training sessions.

**Week 4**

- Collect the baseline data for the flagship (Chapter 6): at least two weeks, measured the same way the pilot will be.
- Build the flagship business case and pilot targets.
- Complete the Tier 2 checklist for the flagship.
- Leadership approves the flagship business case and pilot charter.

**End of Phase 1 deliverables:** an approved AI use policy; a scored use-case list; a business case and pilot charter for the flagship; a quick win underway; an approved assistant available to staff.

## Phase 2: Pilot (Days 31 to 60)

The aim of this phase is to run the flagship pilot with real users and real work, and get the quick win to a visible result.

**Week 5**

- Run a fair vendor test on your own data if you are buying (Chapter 7). Choose.
- Set up the tool for the pilot: connections, instructions, feedback buttons, quality sampling.
- Train the pilot users (half-day session plus floor support).

**Week 6**

- Start the pilot with volunteers.
- Daily check-ins for the first week: what's working, what's annoying, what's wrong.
- Fix the most common problems in the instructions or setup.

**Week 7**

- Expand the pilot to the full pilot group.
- Track adoption by person and team. Act on gaps.
- Share early quick-win results across the company.

**Week 8**

- Mid-pilot review with the sponsor: metrics against targets, issues, any change of course.
- Start the foundation project if it needs a longer run (data cleanup, permissions).
- Broad assistant rollout continues: training sessions, champions active.

**End of Phase 2 deliverables:** a flagship pilot running with weekly metrics; a completed quick win with results shared; champions network in place.

## Phase 3: Prove (Days 61 to 90)

The aim of this phase is to finish the pilot, decide on evidence, and plan what comes next.

**Weeks 9 and 10**

- Pilot continues. Keep measuring after the novelty fades.
- Look beneath the averages: which task types, teams and users benefit, and which don't (Chapter 17).
- Calculate actual checking time and other costs.
- Build the scaling plan and its costs, in case the decision is "scale."

**Week 11**

- Write the pilot results report: all four layers of metrics against targets, what didn't work, and the recommendation: scale, fix and re-test, or stop.
- Update the business case with what you learned.

**Week 12**

- Decision meeting: leadership or the AI council decides.
- Communicate the decision and the reasons to everyone involved, including the pilot users.
- Re-score the use-case list with what you now know. Choose the next one or two use cases.
- Set the review dates for the scaled use case (six and twelve months).

**End of Phase 3 deliverables:** a decision on the flagship, made on evidence; a scaling plan if approved; the next use cases chosen; a plan for the following quarter.

## What Will Go Wrong

Something will. Here are the most common problems and what to do.

- **The data isn't where you thought.** Narrow the pilot to the part of the process where the data is ready, and make the rest a foundation project.
- **Legal or security raises a concern late.** This is why the Tier 2 checklist comes in week 4. If it still happens, pause the specific issue, not the whole pilot.
- **Adoption is low.** Look for the cause: tool friction, wrong instructions, conflicting targets or an unconvinced manager (Chapter 16).
- **The vendor misses dates.** Use the time for training and process design. Hold the vendor to the pilot clause in the contract.
- **A leader wants to skip the pilot and roll out to everyone.** Show them the sensitivity analysis. A failed company-wide rollout costs far more than a 30-day delay.

## The 90-Day Plan for a Small Company

For a company of 10 to 50 people, compress the plan:

- **Weeks 1 and 2:** approve an assistant, write a half-page policy, and pick one use case in one meeting.
- **Weeks 3 to 8:** run it with the people who do the work. Measure before and after with simple counts and times.
- **Weeks 9 to 12:** decide whether to keep it, and pick the next one.

The principle doesn't change: one use case, measured, decided on evidence, then the next.

## Key Points

- Appoint a program lead with real time before you start.
- Decide (days 1 to 30): policy, assistant, scored use cases, flagship business case, quick win.
- Pilot (days 31 to 60): choose the vendor, run the pilot with volunteers first, fix fast, track adoption.
- Prove (days 61 to 90): measure honestly, decide to scale, fix or stop, and choose what's next.
- Expect problems and handle them without abandoning the discipline.

## Questions for Your Leadership Team

1. Who will be our AI program lead, and what will they stop doing to make time?
2. What is our flagship, quick win and foundation for the next 90 days?
3. What date will we make the scale-or-stop decision, and who decides?
4. What would make us skip a step, and how will we resist that temptation?
5. How will we communicate progress to the whole company during the 90 days?
