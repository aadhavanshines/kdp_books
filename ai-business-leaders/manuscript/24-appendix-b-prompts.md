# Appendix B: Prompts for Leaders

These prompts support the personal AI habit in Chapter 3 and the AI program in Parts II and IV. Replace the text in [brackets] with your own details. Use them only in an AI tool your company has approved, and leave out confidential details unless that tool is approved for them.

Each prompt is a starting point. If the first answer isn't right, tell the assistant what to change: "shorter," "more skeptical," "use our numbers, not examples," "give me three options instead of one."

## Thinking and Deciding

**Prompt 1: Stress-test a decision**

```
I am about to decide [decision]. My reasoning is: [reasoning]. Act as
a skeptical, experienced board member. Give me the five strongest
objections, the evidence that would answer each, and the one question
I should not leave the room without answering.
```

**Prompt 2: Pre-mortem**

```
Imagine it is one year from now and [project or decision] has failed
badly. Write the most likely story of how it failed, in five short
steps. Then list the three early warning signs we should watch for,
and one action for each that would reduce the risk now.
```

**Prompt 3: Options I haven't considered**

```
Here is a problem we face: [problem]. We are currently considering
[option A] and [option B]. Suggest three other options a thoughtful
competitor might choose, with the main advantage and main risk of
each. Be concrete and realistic for a company of [size] in
[industry].
```

**Prompt 4: Second-order effects**

```
If we [planned change], what are the likely effects on customers,
employees, suppliers and competitors in the first three months and
after a year? Separate the likely effects from the possible ones,
and flag any effect that could reverse the benefit.
```

## Reading and Research

**Prompt 5: Executive summary of a long document**

```
Summarize this document for a busy executive in under 200 words.
Then list: the three points most relevant to [our company / my
question], any figures I should check before repeating, and one claim
I should be skeptical of, with the reason.
```

**Prompt 6: Compare two documents**

```
Compare these two documents [for example, two proposals or two
versions of a policy]. Give me a table of the main differences: the
topic, what document 1 says, what document 2 says, and why the
difference matters to us. Quote the relevant wording.
```

**Prompt 7: Learn a topic fast**

```
Explain [topic] to me as if I were a smart executive with no
background in it. Use examples from [industry]. Keep it under 500
words. Then give me ten questions to test whether I've understood it,
and five questions I should ask an expert.
```

**Prompt 8: What customers are really saying**

```
Here are [number] customer comments from [source]. Group them into
themes, with a count for each theme and two short quotes that
represent it. Then list the three themes that most need action, and
anything surprising. Do not invent comments or counts.
```

## Writing and Communicating

**Prompt 9: A difficult email**

```
Help me write an email to [person/role] about [situation]. What I
need them to understand: [points]. The relationship matters because
[reason]. Tone: [direct / warm / firm but respectful]. Under 200
words. Give me two versions: one more direct, one softer.
```

**Prompt 10: An announcement to staff**

```
Draft a short message to all staff announcing [change]. Explain why,
what will happen and when, what it means for them, and where to ask
questions. Be honest about what we don't know yet. Plain English, no
corporate jargon, under 300 words.
```

**Prompt 11: Make my draft clearer**

```
Here is a draft I wrote: [paste]. Make it clearer and shorter without
changing the meaning or my voice. Then list the changes you made and
why, so I can decide which to keep.
```

**Prompt 12: A board paper outline**

```
I need to write a board paper asking for approval of [decision]. Give
me an outline of no more than one page: the decision required, the
background, the options considered, the recommendation, the costs and
benefits, the risks and how they are managed, and what happens next.
Under each heading, list the questions the board is likely to ask.
```

## Meetings and People

**Prompt 13: Prepare for a one-to-one**

```
I have a one-to-one with [role] about [topic or concern]. Suggest
five open questions that help me understand what is going on without
sounding as if I have already decided. Then suggest how to respond
if they become defensive.
```

**Prompt 14: Prepare for a negotiation**

```
I am negotiating [what] with [counterparty]. Our goals: [goals]. Our
limits: [limits]. What they probably want: [guess]. List their likely
arguments and good responses, the trade-offs we could offer that cost
us little, and the signs the negotiation is going badly.
```

**Prompt 15: Turn notes into actions**

```
Here are my rough notes from a meeting: [paste]. Turn them into a
clean summary: decisions made, actions (owner and date for each), and
open questions. Flag any action without a clear owner.
```

**Prompt 16: Feedback for a direct report**

```
Help me prepare feedback for a direct report. What they did well:
[examples]. What needs to change: [examples]. Help me express this
in specific, fair, kind language, using the
situation-behavior-impact format. Suggest one question to open the
conversation and one to close it.
```

## Strategy and Planning

**Prompt 17: SWOT with evidence**

```
Here is information about our business and market: [facts]. Draft a
SWOT analysis. For each point, say whether it comes from the facts I
gave you or is your assumption. Then suggest the two strategic
questions this analysis raises most strongly.
```

**Prompt 18: Competitor view**

```
Based on this public information about [competitor]: [paste], what
does their strategy appear to be? Where might they be vulnerable, and
where are we vulnerable to them? Separate what the information shows
from what you are inferring.
```

**Prompt 19: Turn a strategy into goals**

```
Our strategy for next year is: [summary]. Suggest five measurable
goals that would show whether we are succeeding, with a metric, a
baseline we would need to measure, and a realistic target for each.
Flag any goal that might encourage the wrong behavior.
```

**Prompt 20: Scenario planning**

```
Help me plan for three scenarios over the next 18 months for a
[industry] business: demand rises 10 percent, stays flat, falls 15
percent. For each, list the early signs, the decisions we would need
to make, and when we would need to make them.
```

## Running the AI Program

**Prompt 21: Find use cases in a department**

```
I run a [industry] business with [size]. The [department] team of
[number] people handles [main work, with volumes if known]. List ten
ways AI could help this team, each in one sentence: "AI could [do
what] so that [business result], measured by [metric]." Mark each as
high, medium or low volume, and flag any that involve decisions about
individuals.
```

**Prompt 22: Challenge a business case**

```
Here is a business case for an AI project: [paste or summarize]. Act
as a skeptical CFO. Which benefits are hard (they change a budget
line) and which are soft? Which costs are missing? Which assumption
matters most? What would you need to see from a pilot before
approving the full rollout?
```

**Prompt 23: Vendor questions**

```
We are evaluating an AI product for [use case]. Write 15 questions to
ask the vendor, grouped under: fit and results, integration, data and
security, control and quality, full cost, and the vendor itself.
Include questions about what happens to our data and how we could
leave.
```

**Prompt 24: Design a pilot**

```
Help me design a six-week pilot for [AI use case] in [team]. Suggest:
the baseline metrics to collect first, targets for adoption, quality,
process and business results, how to sample quality, what to do in
week one to get adoption, and the decision rule at the end.
```

**Prompt 25: Review pilot results**

```
Here are the results of our AI pilot: [table of metrics, baseline,
target, result, and notes]. Summarize what the results show, what the
averages might be hiding, what didn't work, and whether the evidence
supports scaling, fixing and re-testing, or stopping. Be candid.
```

**Prompt 26: Draft an AI use policy**

```
Draft a one-page AI use policy for a [industry] company of [size].
Cover: approved tools, information that must never be entered, what
employees must check, when to tell people AI was used, decisions AI
must never make, how to report problems, and who to ask. Plain
English, under 400 words.
```

**Prompt 27: Assess a use case's risk**

```
Here is a proposed AI use case: [description]. Using three tiers
(1: internal and fully checked; 2: reaches customers after human
review, or uses personal data or system connections; 3: acts on its
own or affects decisions about individuals), which tier is it, and
why? List the main risks and a practical control for each.
```

**Prompt 28: Prepare a talk about AI and jobs**

```
I need to talk to [team] about how AI will affect their work. Our
goals: [goals]. Our plans for jobs: [honest summary]. What we will
not do: [commitments]. Help me prepare a five-minute talk that is
honest, specific and calm, and list the hardest questions I am likely
to be asked, with honest answers.
```

## Checking AI Output

**Prompt 29: Check your own answer**

```
Review the answer you just gave. List any statements that might be
wrong, out of date, or not supported by the information I provided.
For each figure, say where it came from. Then give me a corrected
version if needed.
```

**Prompt 30: Argue the other side**

```
You have given me arguments for [position]. Now give me the strongest
honest case against it, as someone who disagrees would put it. Then
tell me which side you think the evidence supports better, and why.
```
