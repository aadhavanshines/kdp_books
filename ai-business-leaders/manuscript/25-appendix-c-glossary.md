# Appendix C: Glossary

**Adoption.** The share of the people or tasks that could use an AI tool which actually do. Usually the biggest driver of an AI business case.

**AI agent.** An AI system that can work through several steps and take actions in other systems, such as looking up an order and rebooking a delivery, not just produce text.

**AI council.** A small group of leaders that approves higher-risk AI use cases, keeps the AI register and reviews results.

**AI register.** A list of every AI use case in pilot or production, with its owner, risk tier, vendor, data used and latest results.

**Baseline.** The measured performance of a process before a change, used to judge whether the change worked.

**Champion.** An employee who uses AI well, helps colleagues and feeds ideas and problems back to the AI program.

**Comparison group.** A team or site that does not yet use an AI tool, measured over the same period as one that does, to separate the tool's effect from other changes.

**Deflection.** The share of customer contacts handled without reaching a person. Only meaningful alongside resolution and re-contact rates.

**Fine-tuning.** Further training of an AI model on specific examples to change its behavior. Rarely needed for business use cases today; clear instructions and access to the right documents usually work better and cost less.

**Generative AI.** AI that produces new content such as text, summaries, images or code, in response to a request.

**Grounding.** Giving an AI model specific source documents or data to base its answer on, which makes answers more accurate and checkable.

**Hallucination.** A confident but false answer produced by an AI model, such as an invented figure, name or reference.

**Large language model (LLM).** The kind of AI model behind most generative AI assistants, trained on very large amounts of text to predict and produce language.

**Predictive AI (machine learning).** AI that learns patterns from historical data to make predictions, such as forecasting demand or flagging likely fraud.

**Prompt.** The instructions and information given to an AI model in a request.

**Prompt injection.** An attack in which text hidden in a document, email or web page tries to give instructions to an AI system. A particular risk for agents that read outside content and can take actions.

**Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG).** A common design in which the system first finds relevant passages in your documents and then gives them to the model to answer from. Most "chat with your documents" tools work this way.

**Risk tier.** A category (in this book, Tier 1 to 3) that decides how much review an AI use case needs, based on its potential for harm.

**Sensitivity analysis.** Changing one assumption in a business case at a time to see how much the result depends on it.

**Shadow AI.** AI tools used or bought by employees or teams without the organization's knowledge or approval.

**Token.** The unit AI providers use to measure and charge for text processed by a model; roughly three-quarters of an English word.

**Use case.** A specific application of AI to a specific task for a specific team, with a business result that can be measured.
