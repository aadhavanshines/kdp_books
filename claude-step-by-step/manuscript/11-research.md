# Chapter 11: Web Search and Research

Claude learned most of what it knows from text gathered up to a certain date. For anything newer, or anything that changes, such as prices, opening hours, laws, news, product models or schedules, it needs to look things up. Claude has two ways to do that: quick **web search** inside a normal answer, and **Research**, which produces a full report.

## Web Search Happens Automatically

You don't need to switch anything on. When a question needs current or specific facts, Claude searches the web by itself. You saw this in Chapter 6: while checking the lease, Claude decided that one Ohio law mattered, searched for it, and quoted it. The gray line **Searched the web** appears in the answer. Click it to see exactly what was searched and which pages were read.

At the end of an answer that used the web, Claude lists its **sources** as links. Click them. Checking one or two sources takes seconds and is the best habit you can build with any AI tool.

### Asking for a search

If you want Claude to check the web, say so:

```prompt
Search the web: what are the current opening hours of the Denver Botanic Gardens in December, and do I need to book a timed ticket? Give me the source.
```

Words that usually lead Claude to search: "current," "latest," "this week," "today's," "2026," "search," "look up," and "check online."

### Getting better search answers

- **Name the place and time.** "In Columbus, Ohio, this winter" gets better results than "near me."
- **Ask for official sources.** "Use the official government or company website if possible."
- **Ask for the date.** "Tell me how recent each source is."
- **Ask what's uncertain.** "If sources disagree, say so."

## Research: A Report in Minutes

**Research** is for bigger questions that need many sources, such as comparing products, understanding a medical condition before a specialist appointment, checking a business idea, or preparing for a big purchase. Claude plans the research, searches many times, reads dozens of pages, and writes a structured report with citations. It takes several minutes instead of seconds.

To use it, open the **+** menu and choose **Research**, then type your question. Research is included in paid plans; check your plan if you don't see it.

```prompt
I'm choosing a robot vacuum for a two-floor house with a long-haired dog and mostly hardwood floors. Budget up to $600. Compare the best current options on pet hair, mapping of two floors, noise, running costs (bags, filters) and reliability complaints. End with a short recommendation and a table.
```

A good research request:

- **States the decision** you're trying to make.
- **Gives your situation** (two floors, long-haired dog, hardwood).
- **Lists what matters** to you, in order.
- **Says what format** you want at the end.

While Research runs, you can leave the chat open and do something else. When it's done, the report appears with numbered citations you can click.

## When to Use Which

| You want... | Use |
| --- | --- |
| A quick current fact: hours, price, date, a rule | Normal chat; Claude searches if needed |
| A few facts with a source to check | Ask Claude to "search the web and give sources" |
| A full comparison or background report | Research from the + menu |
| Information from your own files | Attach files or use a Project, no web needed |

## Checking What Claude Found

Even with sources, Claude can misread a page or rely on an outdated one. For anything that matters, such as money, health, law or travel bookings:

1. Click at least one source and confirm the key fact yourself.
2. Check the date on the source page.
3. For medical, legal and financial questions, use the answer to prepare, then confirm with a professional.

Chapter 23 has a full five-minute checking routine.
