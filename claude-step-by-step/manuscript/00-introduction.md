# Introduction

You have probably already tried Claude. You typed a question, got a long, confident answer, and thought: that's impressive, but now what? Maybe you asked it to write an email and spent ten minutes fixing the draft. Maybe you pasted in a report and got a summary that was accurate but not quite useful. Maybe you heard it can build apps, read your inbox and run tasks on a schedule, and you had no idea where those buttons were.

This book closes that gap. It shows you, one step at a time, how to get **finished work** out of Claude: the email you can send, the summary your boss can read in a minute, the spreadsheet that already has the formulas in it, the small app that runs on your phone. Not a draft you have to fix. The finished thing.

## Who This Book Is For

This book is for anyone who uses a computer or a phone and wants Claude to save them real time. You do not need any technical background. There is no code to write anywhere in this book. If you can write an email, you can do everything here.

It is written for:

- **Beginners** who have opened Claude once or twice and want a clear path from "hello" to useful.
- **Busy professionals** who want Claude to take over the writing, reading and admin that fills their week.
- **Curious users** who have heard about Projects, Artifacts, memory, connectors, Skills and Cowork, and want to know what each one does and when to use it.

## What Makes This Book Different

Many guides give you a list of prompts and leave you to work out the rest. This book does three things differently.

**Every workflow shows the real answer.** For each of the 50 workflows you will see the exact request, Claude's actual answer (trimmed only where noted, to save space), and what I changed or asked next to make it finished. You can see what "good" looks like before you try it yourself.

**Every feature is shown, not just named.** Real screenshots of the Claude app, with numbered labels, show you where each button is and what each screen does. A few settings screens, which show private details, are drawn as simple labeled diagrams instead.

**Everything builds into habits.** A workflow you use once saves you ten minutes. A workflow you save in a Project or a Skill, or put on a schedule, saves you ten minutes every week for the rest of the year. Each workflow ends with a "Make it repeatable" note that tells you how.

## How the Book Is Organized

**Part I: Getting Started** (Chapters 1 to 5) explains what Claude is, sets you up on the web, desktop and phone, walks you through your first conversation, and teaches the one habit that makes the biggest difference: describing the finished result you want, using a simple four-part formula.

**Part II: The Features, Step by Step** (Chapters 6 to 15) takes each feature in turn: files and photos, documents and spreadsheets, Artifacts, Projects, memory and incognito, web search and Research, connectors, skills and plugins, tasks and scheduled work, and Claude on your phone, in Chrome and in your office apps. Each chapter ends with a short exercise.

**Part III: 50 Real Workflows** (Chapters 16 to 22) is the heart of the book: worked examples for writing, reading and research, office work, learning, home and money, building small tools without code, and weekly routines that run themselves.

**Part IV: Safe and Smart** (Chapters 23 to 25) covers privacy, checking Claude's work, fixing common problems, and where to go next.

The appendices give you a request library to copy from, a plain-English glossary, and a one-page settings checklist.

You do not need to read the book in order. If you are brand new, read Part I first. After that, jump to whichever feature or workflow you need today.

## A Note on Editions and Changing Screens

Claude changes quickly. In 2026 alone Anthropic released new models, merged Cowork into everyday chat, added editable memory topics, and brought documents, slide decks and designs to every plan. This book describes Claude as it was in **November 2026**.

When a button moves or a menu is renamed, the ideas in this book still apply. Chapter 24 includes a short guide called "If your screen looks different," which shows you how to find any feature in a few seconds. I also avoid relying on exact button colors and positions; instead I tell you what each control does, so you can recognize it even after a redesign.

> **Note:** Some features depend on your plan, your country and your device. Where that matters, the book says so. For current prices and limits, check claude.com/pricing.

## A Note on the Examples

The people and businesses in the examples are fictional. The answers are real: every "Claude's Answer" box was written by Claude in response to that exact request, trimmed only where marked, and the screenshots show the real Claude app. When you try the same request, your answer will be worded differently, because Claude does not give the same reply twice. That is normal, and the book shows you how to steer any answer toward what you need.
