# Chapter 29: Your Study Plan

Knowing what to study matters less than studying it consistently and in the right way. This chapter gives three plans, for two, six and twelve weeks, and the practice habits that make the hours count. Adjust them to your situation: the plans assume about one to two hours on weekdays and a longer session at the weekend.

## How to Practise a Problem

Most candidates practise by reading solutions. It feels productive, but it builds recognition, not recall. In an interview you have to produce a solution, so practise producing them:

1. **Set a timer for 25 to 30 minutes.** Read the problem only, and cover the rest of the page.
2. **Talk aloud** through the six steps of Chapter 2, even when alone. Speaking feels strange at first; it's a skill, and it improves with practice.
3. **Write the code by hand or in a plain editor**, without autocomplete or running it, at least some of the time. Then run it against the examples.
4. **Compare with the book's solution.** Note what you missed: the idea, an edge case, the complexity, or a cleaner way to write it.
5. **Log the problem** in a simple table: date, pattern, solved or not, and the one thing you learned.
6. **Repeat it later.** Return to any problem you couldn't solve after three days, and again after two weeks. Spaced repetition is what moves a pattern from "I've seen it" to "I can do it".

> **Tip:** If you're stuck for more than 10 minutes with no ideas, read only the "Think first" hint and continue. If you're still stuck after 25 minutes, read the idea, not the code, then write the code yourself.

## The Twelve-Week Plan

For career changers, students preparing for their first interviews, or anyone returning after years away. It's the six-week plan at half the speed, with additional practice:

- **Weeks 1 to 2:** Part I slowly, plus any Python basics you need. Solve the Chapter 5 problems twice.
- **Weeks 3 to 10:** two pattern chapters per week. At the end of each week, pick two problems from earlier chapters at random and solve them again, timed.
- **Week 6 and week 10:** a mock interview with another person. Swap roles: being the interviewer teaches you what interviewers notice.
- **Weeks 11 to 12:** Part III, all four mocks, system design if your level requires it, and the story bank.

## Practising With Other People

Solo practice can't fully prepare you for being watched while you think. Ways to add an audience:

- **A study partner:** take turns as interviewer, using the mocks in Chapter 28 as a model. The interviewer reads the problem aloud, answers clarifying questions, gives hints only when needed, and writes notes like the ones at the end of each mock.
- **Record yourself** solving a problem aloud, and watch it back. It's uncomfortable and very effective.
- **Explain a solution** to someone less experienced. If you can't explain why it works, you don't fully own it yet.

## The Week Before and the Day Of

- **Confirm the format** with your recruiter: the number of rounds, languages allowed, the coding environment, and whether AI assistance is permitted in any round.
- **Prepare your setup** for virtual interviews: a quiet room, a reliable connection, a charged laptop, and the coding tool tested in advance.
- **Don't cram new topics** in the last two days. Review your log, Appendix A, and your stories.
- **Sleep.** Problem solving degrades sharply when you're tired.
- **During the interview,** if you get stuck, say what you're thinking and what you're trying. Silence is the one thing an interviewer can't evaluate.
- **After each round,** let it go. You can't judge your own performance accurately, and the next round deserves your full attention.

## If You Don't Get the Offer

Most successful candidates have been rejected before, often by the same companies that later hired them. Ask the recruiter for any feedback they can share; many companies allow you to reapply after a waiting period, often six months to a year. Use the time to work on the weakest area your log shows, and keep interviewing elsewhere: every interview is practice for the next one.

## Key Takeaways

- Practise producing solutions, aloud and timed, not reading them.
- Keep a problem log and re-solve failures after a few days and again after two weeks.
- Choose the plan that matches your time: two, six or twelve weeks.
- Practise with other people, and take turns as interviewer.
- Confirm the format with your recruiter, prepare your setup, and rest before the day.
